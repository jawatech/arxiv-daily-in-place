##### **OmniAct: One Policy, One Million Embodiments**
2611.33158v1 by Xiang Fan, Yuheng Wang, Jiayuan Yan, Bohan Fang, Wisdom Oluchi Ikezogwo, Ainaz Eftekhar, Max Argus, Jason Ren, Jiafei Duan, Ranjay Krishna

A single policy that controls diverse robots must not only transfer task knowledge across embodiments, but also adapt its behavior to each robot's capabilities. Existing approaches facilitate transfer through shared action representations, but these representations do not necessarily capture how differences in robot morphology should shape manipulation strategies. We introduce OmniAct, a cross-embodiment manipulation policy that conditions directly on each robot's kinematics in a specific scene to produce embodiment-specific behavior. This kinematic graph represents the robot's joint positions, motion axes, and connectivity to its visual observation, allowing the policy to account for how its embodiment related to objects and surfaces in the scene. A flow-matching policy predicts relative joint motions for each node, enabling control across robots with different degrees of freedom and kinematic configurations. To learn how physical structure shapes behavior at scale, we procedurally design and filter one million physically feasible robot embodiments; next, we develop algorithms to generate robot trajectories in simulation for all embodiments; finally, we train across diverse simulated manipulation tasks. On 2,000 embodiment-scene-held-out evaluation episodes, OmniAct achieves 63.0% success, compared to 17.8% for pi 0.5 and 57.9% for the strongest baseline; on the task families GPT-6 Astra was evaluated on, it achieves 64.7% against GPT-6 Astra's 41.1%. On a previously unseen real-world robot, it achieves nearly twice the success rate of the next best method, MolmoAct2. Beyond task success, we observe that OmniAct learns distinct manipulation strategies that exploit the capabilities and accommodate the limitations of different morphologies. ##### **MetaFORGE: A Meta-Learning Framework for Harness Optimization in Automated Code Generation for Parallel Computing**
2611.33119v1 by Scott Piersall, Yang Gao, Shenyang Liu, Liqiang Wang

Large language models (LLMs) can generate parallel programs, but their correctness and performance depend strongly on the instructions that guide generation. We present \textsc{MetaFORGE}, an execution-grounded meta-learning framework that iteratively optimizes persistent natural-language skill specifications for MPI C code generation. \textsc{MetaFORGE} combines correctness testing, compiler diagnostics, static-analysis findings, runtime measurements, and rank-level load-imbalance profiles into a scalar reward that drives textual-gradient refinement, while an external memory distills and retrieves reward-supported optimization knowledge across runs.
  Across six MPI tasks and three workload sizes, \textsc{MetaFORGE} improves static-analysis quality, runtime, and load balance relative to zero-shot generation and generally exceeds the evaluated \textsc{TextGrad} iterative baseline. A model-matched refinement ablation shows that the MetaFORGE advantage persists when its refinement model is reduced to \texttt{gpt-4o}. An expanded retrieval ablation across all 18 task--workload configurations shows consistent runtime benefit and broad static-analysis and load-balance benefit from retrieved context. Leakage-controlled held-out and zero-update experiments further provide evidence that optimization knowledge persists in both the transferred skill and external memory under the evaluated protocol.

##### **Uncertainty-Aware Candidate Generation for Entity Linking in Cyber Threat Intelligence**
2611.33086v1 by Nasser Alkhulaifi, Direnc Pekaslan, Christian Wagner

Entity Resolution (ER) pipelines often collapse ambiguity into early hard decisions: upstream Named Entity Recognition (NER) selects a single span and type, and downstream Entity Linking (EL) treats that choice as ground truth. This discards valuable information about the ambiguity of the underlying data (e.g., span boundary disagreement and type confusion), leading to brittle systems that fail unpredictably when faced with uncertainty in inputs. Therefore, this work presents a proof-of-concept for uncertainty-aware ER that treats uncertainty as native data that should be preserved and exploited throughout ER pipelines. We (i) capture uncertainty signals in the NER stage and (ii) leverage these signals into the EL stage to improve candidate retrieval under ambiguity.
