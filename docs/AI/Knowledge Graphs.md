##### **On the Reliability of Majority-Vote Pseudo-Labels in Test-Time Reinforcement Learning**
2611.09872v1 by Haoyu Wang, Ru Wang, Yuanyuan Wang, Jiaxian Guo, Mingming Gong

Test-time reinforcement learning (TTRL) adapts pretrained language models to unlabeled target-domain data using self-generated pseudo-labels, whose reliability remains poorly understood. We analyze majority-vote supervision for TTRL with online Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and decompose its error into population-majority bias and prompt-dependent finite-rollout variation. Because population-majority bias is generally unidentifiable without external supervision, we focus on mitigating the downstream effects of finite-rollout variation. We propose \emph{Prompt-Conditional Distributionally Robust DPO (PC-Dr.DPO)}, which conditions within-prompt robust reweighting on observed vote reliability to make updates from sampling-sensitive prompts more conservative. We further introduce a cache-based mechanism to reduce self-supervised forgetting during online adaptation. Across six mathematical and knowledge-intensive reasoning benchmarks and five model backbones, PC-Dr.DPO matches or outperforms the strongest TTRL baseline in 24 of 30 settings.

##### **GRAC: Graph-Based Retrieval-Augmented Captioning for Surgical Scene Description**
2611.09866v1 by Rochak Dhakal, Chen Zhao, Soup Cobb, Xin Qian, Xiaofeng Yang, Weihua Zhou

Purpose: Surgical operative reports support procedural documentation, surgical training, performance assessment, and workflow review. However, extracting meaningful information often requires extensive manual review and clinical expertise. Automated surgical scene descriptions could reduce this burden by leveraging recurring surgical activities and prior knowledge.
  Methods: We propose GRAC (Graph-Based Retrieval-Augmented Captioning), a framework that represents surgical scenes as graphs capturing relationships among instruments, actions, and anatomical structures. Given an input scene graph, GRAC learns to retrieve relevant graph-caption pairs from training data. It compares instrument-tissue interactions between retrieved examples and the input scene, using comparison features to condition retrieved-caption representations during generation.
  Results: On the EndoVis-18 benchmark, GRAC achieved a CIDEr-D of 5.88, compared with 4.23 for SGT and 3.36 for the strongest conventional Transformer baseline. Exact instrument-action-target triplet-set accuracy reached 52.6%, compared with 32.7% for SGT and 26.2% for the strongest conventional baseline. These results demonstrate improvements in language quality and factual agreement. On TORS, GRAC achieved a CIDEr-D of 5.06, compared with 3.80 for the strongest baseline.
  Conclusion: GRAC demonstrates that combining structured surgical scene representations with retrieved examples improves surgical scene captioning and produces more contextually meaningful descriptions.

##### **Discovering formally verified quantum programs with AI**
2611.09822v1 by Sergey Shirobokov, Ben Jaderberg, Katerina Hristova, George Tsoukalas, Johannes Bausch, Eric Wieser, Francisco J. H. Heras, Nicholas C. Rubin, Guang Hao Low, Sébastien Racanière, Nenad Tomašev, Lei M. Zhang, James Watson, Tanuj Khattar, Aws Albarghouthi, Stavan Jain, Harun Khan, Leopoldo Sarra, Andrew W. Senior, Michael Broughton, Hussain Masoom, Sergio Boixo, Ryan Babbush, Swarat Chaudhuri, Pushmeet Kohli

The timeline by which quantum computers can solve breakthrough problems in science depends on improved quantum algorithms as much as on better hardware. Such algorithmic advances are typically achieved through many years of expert human collaboration. In mathematics and computer science, large language models have accelerated progress through automated discovery loops. However, quantum algorithms can only be evaluated by classical computers on restricted or small instances. Here we demonstrate automated discovery of quantum programs beyond these regimes including order-of-magnitude improvements on scientific applications. To achieve this, we build a quantum program library in the Lean theorem prover in which large language model agents synthesize novel programs against formal problem specifications and verify their correctness and cost. Agent teams collaborate, sharing results and knowledge through wikis and discussing ideas at organized conferences. Our results improve on prior baselines for parallel search (up to 1.4x), quantum arithmetic (290x), and simulations relevant to fusion, photochemistry and drug-metabolism by 11x, 8x and 2.5x respectively. We introduce FrontierPhysics, an end-to-end benchmark with 56 tasks made by 42 physicists based on their own research, covering nine domains across theoretical, computational, and experimental physics. ##### **LongBanana: An Expert-Verified Benchmark for Long-Context Multi-Reference Image Synthesis**
2611.06856v1 by Haoxiang Cao, Yuxuan Zhang, Penghui Du, Bo Li, Jiajiong Cao, Yingying Fan, Zimeng Wu, Yifu Luo, Mingzhe Zheng, Junwen Miao, Huaisong Zhang, Yang Yao, Yu Wang, Dongfu Jiang, Ping Nie, Wenhu Chen, Chaoqun Wang, Changqian Yu, Kelsey R. Allen

We introduce LongBanana, to our knowledge the first expert-designed and expert-verified benchmark for long-context multi-reference image synthesis in the age of agent-driven image generation. The task requires retrieving dispersed visual evidence, rejecting distractors, binding entities and relations, and composing one coherent final image from multiple references. LongBanana contains 90 carefully designed tasks organized by a 3-by-3 taxonomy; each task includes about 32 reference images, 20 constraints, and 50 evaluation items, yielding 4,670 expert-specified atomic evaluation items in total. Its graph-grounded evaluation design instantiates each task with reference images and an entity-relation graph, then converts node, edge, and whole-graph obligations into checklist items for Content Fidelity, Relational Binding, and Global Constraint Satisfaction. Reviewed expert labels include a 15% double-labeled subset with 89.4% exact agreement and 0.83 quadratic weighted kappa. We further introduce LongBanana-Gen, an agent-harness framework for long-horizon image generation; experiments show that minimal agent harnesses improve over direct generation, while a ReAct-Plan-Exec-Reflect (RPR) workflow yields harness-dependent gains by strengthening evidence grounding and repair. Together, LongBanana provides an auditable testbed for measuring dense visual evidence use and a shared target for developing stronger agent-driven image-generation systems. Code and data are available at the project page: https://long-banana.com/.

##### **LLMs Don't Know What They Know: Benchmarking Temporal Self-Knowledge for Financial Backtesting**
2611.06808v1 by Lingfei Qian, Yan Wang, Xueqing Peng, Yupeng Cao, Yuechen Jiang, Zhuohan Xie, Anke Xu, Wenbo Cao, Jiarui Chen, Zehan Li, Jimin Huang, Alejandro Lopez-Lira, Yuehua Tang, Sophia Ananiadou, Jian-Yun Nie

Financial backtests of large language models (LLMs) are meaningful only if the model did not already know the outcomes it is asked to predict. Practitioners guard against this by trusting a vendor's stated knowledge cutoff, by asking the model what it knows, or by instructing it to answer as of a past date. To verify whether any of these safeguards can be trusted, we introduce FinDendro, a benchmark of 2,226 dated U.S. facts from 1999 to 2026, covering monthly macroeconomic series, month-end stock prices and Federal Reserve decisions. Each fact yields four prompts: a value question, Recall and Predict questions that pair each candidate value with a claim of knowing or guessing without a declared date, and a Predict question with a historical declared date. Controls for persistence and dominant outcomes keep chance accuracy near 0.27. Results show that for most frontier models, self-reports understate knowledge. GPT-5 answers 76% of controlled facts correctly but claims to know 38%, and answers 61% of the facts it says it does not know. Knowledge claims can persist beyond the measured knowledge boundary, by up to seven quarters, and a declared date removes most of the claims of prior knowledge while leaving accuracy unchanged. Backtests inside a model's training window therefore cannot be certified leak-free by self-report, instruction or stated cutoff. These findings motivate behavioral measurement of temporal knowledge boundaries, and FinDendro provides a benchmark and procedure for selecting backtest periods based on demonstrated factual knowledge.

##### **Route First, See Later: Enabling Vision Agents to Reason Scientifically by Separating Routing Policy from Action Spaces**
2611.06760v2 by Shubhabrata Mukherjee, Jeffrey Donatelli, Daniela Ushizima

Scientific image-analysis workflows require agents to select among diverse capabilities like description, detection, counting, and segmentation. Current multi-tool architectures conflate intent interpretation with visual execution, masking whether a failure arises from flawed reasoning (misrouting) or failed perception (invalid tool execution). This lack of auditability renders agents unreliable for high-stakes scientific automation. We introduce ZETA, an extensible architecture that operationalizes the decoupling of routing policy from action execution. ZETA resolves scientific intent before invoking specialized visual tools, treating measurement as a deterministic operation grounded in explicit geometry rather than generative output. This architecture exposes the "black box" of agent decision-making, allowing routing correctness and downstream execution to be evaluated as independent failure modes.
  To our knowledge, we provide the first controlled study in scientific vision-agent routing that isolates explicit routing-policy specification while holding the legal action interface fixed. Evaluation on a zero-shot benchmark of 51 scientific-intent queries shows that explicit routing semantics can substantially improve routing accuracy, particularly at semantically adjacent action boundaries, although improvements vary across models. For Qwen 3.8, explicit routing-policy specification improves segmentation routing accuracy from 6.2% to 75.0% and detection routing accuracy from 60.0% to 100%. ZETA's execution traces make routing decisions and downstream artifacts separately observable, providing a basis for distinguishing intent-selection errors from visual execution failures. ZETA establishes the necessary infrastructure to isolate execution errors from interpretive ones, a critical requirement for deploying reliable scientific vision agents.

##### **Reasoning over Non-Parametric Memory in Multilingual LLMs: A Cognitive-Based Analysis of Relational Knowledge**
2611.06715v1 by Zohreh Abbasi Liasi, Batoul Hammoud, Anas Madkoor, Ehsaneddin Asgari

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) assumes that language models can reason over relations stated in their context rather than fall back on associations memorized during pretraining. We study this evidence-conditioned relational reasoning in isolation, with the facts supplied directly in the prompt rather than retrieved. \textsc{Rel-Bloom} is a controlled multilingual diagnostic of 450 multiple-choice questions built from 30 Wikidata relations; the same source relations are instantiated at five Bloom-inspired levels (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate) and under three veracity conditions (Factual, Counterfactual, Neutralized). Across knowledge conditions, Counterfactual contexts are generally harder than Factual ones, while removing entity identities can improve higher-level performance in some model regimes. Greater model capability therefore does not by itself ensure reliable reasoning over supplied relational evidence. Relevant resources are available at: https://huggingface.co/spaces/QCRI/non-parametric-memory-relational-reasoning.

##### **Beyond the principle-practice gap: Responsible AI as situated epistemic and ethical practice**
2611.06709v1 by Thao Do Ngoc, Emma Carmel

Responsible AI scholarship has found that ethical principles are frequently not reflected in AI design and development. Conceptualised as a `gap' between principle and practice, this is framed as a problem of implementation, awareness or compliance, and by implication, a deficit of systems or individual behaviour. This paper sets out an alternative explanation. Drawing on fourteen semi-structured interviews with actors designing and governing AI-enabled anti-trafficking systems across law enforcement, commercial, research, and survivor-support contexts, it argues that such apparent gaps emerge from the varied and complex institutional and professional conditions through which actors come to recognise some issues, but not others, as ethical problems requiring action. We show that epistemic choices are intertwined with ethical judgements, and trace four interconnected moments---perception, judgement, situated action and socio-technical materialisation---through which concerns become intelligible, are weighed and acted upon, and are embedded in technical artefacts and governance arrangements. Actors often hold genuine ethical commitments, but these commitments are shaped by institutional logics and professional moral economies: some concerns are materialised, while others are narrowed, deferred or left unrealised. We show that ethical agency is exercised within and between competing constraints of profession, role, prior experience, and institutional settings. The paper argues that responsible AI governance requires purposeful and well-developed epistemic infrastructures that can cut across these constraints to establish institutional conditions through which the knowledge of affected communities and potential harms becomes receivable and actionable in design.

##### **Human Motion Prediction via Continual Prior Compensation**
2611.06637v1 by Jianwei Tang, Jian-Fang Hu, Tianming Liang, Xiaotong Lin, Jiangxin Sun, Wei-Shi Zheng, Jianhuang Lai

Human Motion Prediction (HMP) aims to predict future human poses at different moments according to observed past motion sequences. Previous approaches mainly treated the prediction of different temporal moments as a single prediction task and learned the predictions of varied moments simultaneously, which would encounter a main limitation: the learning of short-term predictions (referring to ``near-future'' prediction) could be hindered by the predictions of long-term (referring to ``far-future'' prediction) motions. In this paper, we develop a novel temporal continual learning framework called Continual Prior Compensation (CPC) to progressively train HMP models, in which we divide the prediction task of motions corresponding to varied temporal moments into several subtasks and train the model in a multi-stage manner. To mitigate the prior information forgetting in the progressive training, we further introduce a learnable random variable Prior Compensation Factor (PCF) to explicitly measure the prior knowledge loss. We theoretically show that the PCF can be efficiently learned together with the model parameters by minimizing a reasonable upper bound of the objective function. The proposed CPC is further enhanced to estimate the prior information loss for each subtask and a new framework called Continual Prior Compensation++ (CPC++) with Fine-Grained Prior Compensation Factor (FGPCF) is finally developed. Our CPC and CPC++ frameworks are quite flexible and can be easily integrated with different HMP backbone models and adapted to various datasets and applications. Extensive experiments on three HMP benchmark datasets using multiple SOTA HMP backbones (PGBIG, siMLPe, MotionMixer, and LTD) demonstrate the effectiveness and flexibility of our frameworks.

##### **NaijaFacts: A Framework for Disentangling Language from Knowledge in Facts about Nigeria**
2611.06504v1 by Zainab Afolabi, Nikita Kozodoi, Jack Butler, Babs Khalidson

Multilingual benchmarks for African languages mostly translate generic questions into local languages, measuring linguistic competence but not whether models know facts about Africa. We introduce \naija, a benchmark of factual and statistical questions about Nigeria, authored by native speakers and grounded in authoritative official statistics, with every question available in English, Nigerian Pidgin, \yoruba, Igbo, and Hausa. Answering similar questions for the UK and US yields a country by language matrix that disentangles a language effect (the model knows the fact but degrades in a low-resource language) from a knowledge effect (the fact is absent in every language). Across eight frontier and Nigeria-adapted models, the knowledge effect dominates: English-query accuracy is 49--50\% for the US/UK but 20\% for Nigeria. The language effect is smaller and tracks web representation, degrading from English to \yoruba{} (9 points on Nigeria, up to 24 on US/UK questions). We find scale does not help: the strongest models reach 66--79\% on the UK and the US but none exceed 31\% on Nigerian statistics. Extending to all 54 African countries with our best performing frontier model shows the same profile continent-wide, 96\% on culture and 98\% on politics against 26\% on statistics, so the gap is not specific to Nigeria.

##### **ArcRAG: Agentic Compositional Retrieval over Knowledge Graphs**
2611.06481v1 by Jyotin Goel, Abhinav Arun, Tejas Prakash Agarwal, Bhaskarjit Sarmah, Stefano Pasquali

Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation (GraphRAG) answers questions by retrieving over a knowledge graph built from a corpus. Yet retrieval strategies are designed around the structure their own construction pipeline produces, and whether they survive a change of construction is rarely tested. We study this cross-construction portability: whether one retrieval procedure, with no construction-specific schema, relation names, or retuning, stays effective when the graph is built differently. We introduce ArcRAG, which induces a query-local graph interface at inference time (the node labels, relation descriptors, and property keys reachable from the entities a question names), plans multi-hop traversals against it, and executes and repairs them as Cypher. We build each of the MuSiQue, HotpotQA, and TriviaQA corpora as a schema-guided graph (typed nodes, a closed relation set, source provenance) and as an open-information-extraction graph (untyped nodes, free-text relations), with the retriever's configuration fixed across them. We compare against a construction-agnostic Direct-Cypher agent, ablate each stage across retry and pass budgets on both graph families, and compare with results reported for prior GraphRAG systems, most of which design retrieval together with their own graph construction. Across the two constructions, we find that retrieval need not be co-designed with the graph: query-time interface induction keeps a construction-agnostic retriever effective when the construction changes, although construction still shapes absolute accuracy.
