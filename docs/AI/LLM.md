##### **WeightBench: Evaluating Language Models as Weight-Space Programmers**
2611.18553v1 by Aayush Marasini, Barsat Khadka, Nick Rahimi

Recent work shows that large language models (LLMs) can operate directly on the parameters of other neural networks. They can read what a network was trained to do, fix a network that fails, and write working networks from scratch, all without gradient descent. This capability has been termed Weight-Space Programming (WSP). Exploring this new paradigm systematically requires a robust benchmark. We introduce WEIGHTBENCH, a benchmark that evaluates LLMs of increasing capability on 42 WSP tasks across five tracks: reading, fixing and writing weights, recognizing edits that are impossible, and data-free compression. The networks whose weights the LLMs read and edit range from 2K-parameter control policies to 7B-parameter language models. Experimental benchmark results show that frontier agents can already read small networks and write working control policies without ever running them, but cannot yet patch language models, compress them without data, or tell an impossible edit from a hard one. On all 42 tasks, Claude Opus 5.5 reaches a macro score of 0.535 (Sonnet 5.5 0.466, Haiku 5.5 0.402, DeepSeek v4-flash 0.215); on the 38 tasks it was run on, GPT-6.1-sol reaches 0.568 against Opus's 0.546. Code, the task registry, our OS-level sandbox harness, and the live leaderboard are publicly available at https://github.com/Aayush-Marasini/WeightBench.

##### **Elliptic Sampling: Optimal Interpolant Schedules for Gaussians and Beyond**
2611.18542v1 by Cole Becker, Taylan Kargin, Kimia Hassibi, Asuman Ozdaglar, Pablo A. Parrilo

Generative modeling based on stochastic interpolants requires specifying two key choices: an interpolant schedule bridging the initial and target distributions, and an efficient discretization of the resulting probability-flow ODE. In this work, we introduce a new framework for joint schedule/discretization design based on the analyticity properties of the (complexified) ODE trajectories, which in turn ensure good numerical behavior. Our formulation can be solved exactly via conformal mapping, yielding a natural family of variance-preserving elliptic schedules, parametrized by Jacobi elliptic functions. Pairing an elliptic schedule with a tailored discretization, we obtain Zoloflow, a new sampling algorithm closely connected to Zolotarev's optimal rational function approximants. Crucially, both the elliptic schedule and Zoloflow explicitly incorporate the condition number $κ$ of the target distribution and reduce to the classical trig schedule in the limit $κ\rightarrow 1$. For Gaussian targets, Zoloflow achieves minimax optimality among all oblivious score-span algorithms, attaining total-variation error $\varepsilon$ using $Θ(\logκ\,\log(\sqrt d/\varepsilon))$ (exact) score queries. For the log-concave case, the elliptic schedule allows sampling with at most $\varepsilon$ 2-Wasserstein-error by requiring merely $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{d} \, \mathrm{polylog}(κ, 1/\varepsilon))$ score evaluations. Complementing these theoretical results, we empirically evaluate the elliptic schedule on general (non-log-concave) target distributions, including detailed comparisons with alternative schedule proposals on both synthetic benchmarks and existing image generation datasets.

##### **ModHarness-RL: Recasting Harnesses as Learnable and Evolving Programs for Agentic RL**
2611.18533v1 by Zelin He, Siyu Zhu, Jacob Helwig, Yujian Liu, Li An, Rohit Jain, Shiyu Chang, Alborz Geramifard

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) increasingly relies on a harness, the scaffold around the model that decides what it sees of its own interaction and what guidance it receives. Most existing approaches train under a hand-written harness that stays fixed throughout. However, an effective harness depends on the domain, the data and the model, and keeps changing as the model trains. We introduce ModHarness-RL, which recasts the harness as programs that are learned from data and evolve as the model trains with RL. ModHarness-RL records the interaction as structured State, a set of typed blocks, and two core components of the harness are programs over these blocks, a Compression program that assembles the accumulated history into the model's context and a Steering program that supplies guidance when behavior deviates. During RL training, candidate programs are proposed from recent trajectories, verified by execution, and replayed over past trajectories, so the harness tracks how the model's behavior shifts. We study CodeSearch in depth and further analyze ALFWorld and BIRD SQL, with models from 1.5B to 8B. The proposed Compression and Steering mechanisms improve held-out performance over the fixed-harness baseline in most settings. These results suggest that a harness for agentic RL can be learned from the task's data and evolved with the model.
