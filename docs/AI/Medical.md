##### **What You Post Is What You Symptom: Estimating Psychometric Networks from Social Media Discourse**
2611.21362v1 by Ştefana-Arina Tăbuşcă, Ana Sabina Uban, Liviu P. Dinu

Symptom networks in psychopathology are estimated almost exclusively from self-report questionnaires, restricting inference to whatever symptom set a given instrument covers. We introduce a framework for symptom-resolved network estimation directly from social media discourse and apply the framework to depression and eating disorders, with a third anorexia corpus as cross-dataset validation. To our knowledge, we are the first to estimate symptom-resolved psychometric networks from social media text, compare them to clinical co-endorsement networks, and replicate transdiagnostic linguistic markers across 3 independently recruited corpora spanning 2 disorders simultaneously. For depression, expression scores correlate with BDI-II totals at $ρ{=}0.434$, 20 of 21 items reach FDR significance, and split-half reliability is 0.935. The Network Comparison Test detects few edge differences between modalities (3-5\% of edges), while bridge symptoms diverge. For eating disorders, the item-level results are exploratory. Identified linguistic features replicate the same direction across all 3 corpora, and they cluster in three construct families.

##### **FM-Recon: Exploring Foundation Model Features for Accelerated Cardiac Cine MRI Reconstruction**
2611.21290v1 by Donghang Lyu, Marius Staring, Ruochen Gao, Jochen Keupp, Hildo J. Lamb, Mariya Doneva

Foundation models have demonstrated strong feature extraction capabilities across various medical imaging tasks, yet their potential for MRI reconstruction remains largely unexplored, particularly within unrolled reconstruction networks and under artifact-corrupted inputs. In this work, we explore whether and how features from pretrained foundation models can benefit accelerated cardiac cine MRI reconstruction. To this end, we propose FM-Recon, a framework that refines and integrates extracted features from frozen image encoder of foundation models into an unrolled reconstruction network. Through extensive experiments on the CMRxRecon2023 dataset, we systematically evaluate foundation model features across different reconstruction settings. Our results show that foundation models can extract informative representations from artifact-corrupted inputs and improve reconstruction performance, particularly at moderate acceleration factors. These findings demonstrate the potential of foundation model features for accelerated MRI reconstruction and provide insights into their effective integration into unrolled reconstruction networks.

##### **A Generative AI-Driven Architecture for Whole-Home Intelligent Services: Edge Models, Memory Management, and Household Applications**
2611.21232v1 by Mo Zhou

Generative artificial intelligence is entering the home. Cloud-based assistants face governance limitations in data transmission, continuous availability, and the reliability of physical actions, which are especially salient in households where devices remain online, data accumulate over time, and commands trigger real-world actions. Using the home local server as a unifying organizing axis, this article develops a design-oriented review framework. Sources from arXiv, CNKI, ACL Anthology, and official documentation were searched through November 2026 and coded into five evidence categories: reviews, system prototypes, benchmarks, HCI and eldercare studies, and official documentation. System designs were mapped onto shared architectural modules, identifying nine recurrent responsibilities of the home local server. Generative models support perceptual interpretation, intent understanding, and candidate planning, while deterministic validation, knowledge-graph constraints, and authorization leases confine candidate actions to an inspectable space. Home control, emotional companionship, and intelligent guarding can be compared within a common framework: they share state and memory but differ in latency, false positive and negative rates, proactivity, and permissions. Existing benchmarks rely on virtual environments and cannot substitute for real-deployment evaluation. Local deployment is not inherently safer, and technical continuity alone does not establish the social or medical effects of companionship and guarding. The article proposes a five-dimensional evaluation framework covering architectural capability, system resources, privacy, safety and reliability, and human experience, together with six design principles and a future research agenda for local-first intelligent home systems.
