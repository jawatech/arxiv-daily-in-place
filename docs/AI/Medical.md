##### **AI-Driven Reproducibility and Analytical Robustness in Scientific Discovery**
2610.23845v1 by Shicheng Liu, Krista Opsahl-Ong, Andrew Drozdov, Julian Killingback, Arnav Singhvi, Ronak Pradeep, Michael Bendersky, James C. Dickerson, Monica S. Lam

Reproducible and analytically robust research findings are essential to scientific progress, but evaluating these properties typically requires costly manual effort. We investigate whether LLM agents can help automate reproduction and robustness analysis, using a medical case study grounded in the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database, a widely used U.S. cancer registry underlying tens of thousands of published studies. First, we introduce SEER-Repro, a benchmark of 20 published SEER-based papers together with the data and resources required to reproduce their findings. Second, we create an AI agent that successfully reproduces 77.4% of 2,190 atomic claims, while providing executable baselines for further analysis. Third, we develop an agentic analysis pipeline to test the robustness of the medical findings by identifying analytical choices underlying each finding, proposing defensible alternatives, and rerunning the analysis to determine whether those choices affect the conclusion. Across published papers in SEER-Repro, our pipeline identifies potentially consequential analytical choices in 9.5% of fully reproduced findings and flags them for expert review. These results show how LLM agents can support scientific review by reproducing findings, systematically testing their robustness, and directing expert attention toward potentially consequential analytical choices.

##### **On the possibilities and limitations of LLM-based multi-agent systems for multi-criteria decision-making in healthcare operations management**
2610.23673v1 by Marco Boresta, Tommaso Giovannelli, Sepehr Namirad, Giulia Riccardi

Multi-criteria models for healthcare operations need weights that express how stakeholders trade off patient waiting time, provider workload, and operating cost, but these weights are difficult to quantify. Teams of large language model (LLM) agents, each prompted to represent a stakeholder, have been used to supply them. We propose a framework for assessing the reliability of LLM-generated preferences as inputs to multi-objective optimization and quantifying how their uncertainty affects decisions. We study explicit preferences by asking agents to rate the importance of the criteria in an outpatient appointment scheduling problem, and implicit preferences by inferring trade-offs from the agents' pairwise choices between emergency department configurations. The computational experiments show that the explicit preferences respond to the clinic's operating conditions and differ across roles, but deliberation among the agents can erase these differences, whereas simple averaging preserves them. The pairwise choices used to reveal the agents' implicit preferences depend largely on which option is presented first, and we find limited evidence that a statistical model using contextual information alongside presentation order predicts the agents' choices on new comparison pairs better than a model using presentation order alone. We then formulate robust weighted-sum optimization models to account for preference uncertainty. In most cases, these models select different schedules or configurations from those obtained using average preference weights. These results indicate that LLM-generated preferences need an audit of this kind before they are used as optimization weights in healthcare decision support.

##### **Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT): Evidence-Based Text Generation via a Closed-Loop Retrieve--Reflect--Revise Pipeline on Edge LLMs**
2610.23311v1 by Fabian Broszio, Michael Hoffmann, Adrian Paschke

Large language models (LLMs) can generate fluent outputs that lack source support or conflict with local operational constraints. We introduce Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT), a modular architecture that organizes generation as a closed Retrieve--Reflect--Revise loop. SRCoT separates environment state, persistent knowledge, accumulated experience, and short-term memory into provenance-labeled stores. BM25 retrieval supplies local context; subsequent passes expose intermediate drafts, missing-information markers, follow-up queries, and optional claim--evidence mappings. These artifacts inform retrieval, revision, clarification, or abstention decisions. We examine the architecture through qualitative, trace-based case studies in two fully synthetic settings: emergency medical services and Munich-Sim, an emergency-operations simulation. Local prototype runs on an Apple M4-family laptop with 128 GB memory illustrate source-labeled documentation, explicit gap marking, and structured dispatch-style outputs. ##### **Coverage-Aware Reasoning with Medical Tokens for Diagnosis Prediction**
2610.10641v1 by Kaisong Zhang, Haotian Fang, Junmeng Zhou, Hang Lv, Yulan Pan, Yanchao Tan

Large language models (LLMs) offer promising potential for next-visit diagnosis prediction, owing to their ability to integrate longitudinal clinical evidence and reason over it in natural language. However, reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning commonly rewards each trajectory according to the correctness of its final answer. In next-visit diagnosis prediction, multiple diagnoses can be simultaneously valid, but independently rewarding one diagnosis per trajectory does not distinguish repeated hits from coverage of different diagnoses. The policy can therefore concentrate on a few correct diagnoses, leaving others uncovered. Meanwhile, LLM tokenizers can split ICD codes into several generic tokens with limited clinical meaning, requiring multiple decoding steps to predict each diagnosis and hindering reasoning over a large disease vocabulary. To address both challenges, we propose CARing, a framework that represents diagnoses with compositional Semantic IDs (SIDs) and optimizes reasoning trajectories for multi-label coverage. Concretely, we first encode ontology-enriched disease semantics into compact SIDs through residual quantization, and ground the resulting SID tokens in natural language and longitudinal EHR contexts through multi-task alignment and reasoning-enriched training to unlock transferable LLM reasoning. CARing further improves unordered multi-label prediction through a coverage reward for reinforcement learning and multi-positive supervision. At inference time, the model supports both efficient direct constrained decoding and multi-chain reasoning with rank fusion. On MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV, CARing exceeds all EHR-trained baselines in weighted F1 and attains the highest top-k recall at every reported cutoff, including R@30 of 46.04% and 46.52% in reasoning mode. Our codes and logs are available at https://github.com/zmlxzyh/CARing-Codes-Logs.
