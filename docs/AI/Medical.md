##### **On the Creation of Object-Centric Event Logs from Patient Journeys**
2611.33074v1 by Saimir Bala, Neven Borak, Maximilian König, Anjo Seidel, Zeinab Soleimani, Lorenzo Famiglini, Lev Petrov, Jonas Witt, Mathias Weske

Interactions between patients and health systems as well as conditions that individuals experience during a disease are represented in patient journeys. Therefore, patient journeys are important assets to investigate how care is being provided, and how health systems can be designed to better suit the needs of patients. While there is initial work on patient journeys from a process mining perspective with traditional case notions, object-centric process mining techniques have yet to be covered. This is unfortunate, since due to the rich structure of objects and their relationships, object-centric process mining approaches are particularly promising in this context. This paper addresses this gap by investigating object-centric event log (OCEL) generation from patient journeys. Based on a list of challenges and requirements, the paper presents an approach for the creation of OCELs from patient journey data. The approach is evaluated on a synthetic patient cohort provided by our industry partner.

##### **Can LLM Judges Catch Unsafe Clinical Actions? A Fault-Injection Study**
2611.33012v1 by Neda Ghafouri

As clinical AI agents begin to interact with electronic health records (EHRs), large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to evaluate their actions. But how reliably can these judges recognize clinically dangerous mistakes, especially when an agent appears to have completed its task successfully? We investigate this question using MedAgentBench by introducing controlled clinical errors into verified agent trajectories. The experiments include 416 faulty trajectories, eight error categories, and six open-weight LLM judges. By comparing each faulty trajectory with its correct counterpart, we measure whether judges genuinely recognize errors rather than simply flagging more actions as unsafe. The findings show that access to clinical evidence significantly improves judge reliability. When shown only the final answer, judges cannot distinguish errors that leave it unchanged, while flagging 56% of correct trajectories. With full trajectory information, the ability to distinguish unsafe actions from correct ones reaches a score of 0.75, and providing actual EHR responses improves discrimination by 0.39. Surprisingly, including the agent's reasoning does not improve overall performance and reduces detection of incorrectly reported clinical values by 0.18, which remain the hardest errors to detect. MedGemma performed worse than the general-purpose model and was the only judge to miss potentially dangerous medication overdoses. We also find that the benchmark's original grader accepts all 38 trajectories with an intentionally skipped laboratory check, while combining LLM judges with a rule-based checker informed by the full EHR record detects 97% of injected faults.

##### **ASCENT: A Benchmark for Evaluating and Advancing Stepwise Diagnostic Reasoning in Large Language Models on Common Clinical Scenarios**
2611.32734v1 by Yera Choi, Yeong Hwa Kim, Jaedeok Lee, Taekang Kim, Sangdoo Yun, Seong-Eun Moon

Large language models (LLMs) excel at medical question answering yet are rarely evaluated on the stepwise diagnostic reasoning that defines real clinical workflows, where impressions are revised as information accumulates. We build Annotated Stepwise Clinical rEasoning for NaturalisTic Diagnosis (ASCENT), a clinician-annotated benchmark and training resource of 3,078 stepwise problems derived from MedQA-USMLE that decomposes each vignette into EMR-aligned steps (Findings, Impression, supporting Rationale), enabling evaluation of intermediate reasoning under incomplete information.
  Experiments and training with ASCENT revealed insights into how current LLMs handle stepwise diagnostic reasoning. Even strong reasoning models that perform well on MedQA-USMLE leave substantial headroom on ASCENT, and general-purpose frontier models trail further, exposing a persistent gap between fully informed and stepwise diagnosis. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-7B and 32B on ASCENT yields measurable F1 gains over both pre-trained and HuatuoGPT-o1 CoT-trained baselines, with gains driven primarily by precision.
