##### **ToolSmith: An Automatic Tool-Generation Framework for Multimodal Search Agents**
2611.31198v1 by Rui Sun, Junhua Huang, Luca Zancato, Md Mofijul Islam, Kai-Wei Chang

Recent multimodal search agents use reasoning, vision, and live web search across many turns to answer questions beyond parametric knowledge by orchestrating a small library of hand-crafted search and visual tools. However, the library is laborious to create and maintain, and frozen when new query types, evidence formats, and failure modes are encountered in the wild. Human practitioners, by contrast, cope with insufficient tools by fashioning new ones from familiar parts and reusing them across tasks. Inspired by this, we propose ToolSmith, an automatic tool-generation framework that grows and reuses modules on top of a lean base tool library. We evaluate ToolSmith on three in-domain multimodal search benchmarks and ten out-of-domain benchmarks under a VLM-as-a-Judge protocol. ToolSmith consistently improves over the base library across deployed agents of varying scales, with larger backbones reaping greater gains, demonstrating the scalability of the approach. Our code and data are available at https://threesr.github.io/toolsmith/.

##### **SRCWDNet: Semantic-Relational Cross-Window Dual-Graph Network for Multi-Label Remote Sensing Scene Classification**
2611.31135v1 by Fareeha Amir

Multi-label remote sensing scene classification remains a challenging task due to complex spatial-spectral patterns, extreme class imbalance, and inter-label dependencies that are often insufficiently modeled. To address these challenges, we introduce a Semantic-Relational Cross-Window Dual-Graph Network (SRCWDNet), which integrates three key modules: (1) a Cross-Window Attention module that captures local and global visual context through learnable global tokens; (2) a Dual Label-Graph module that adaptively combines statistical co-occurrence graphs and learned semantic-embedding graphs to facilitate label-relational reasoning; and (3) a Neural Classifier Chain that sequentially predicts labels to exploit inter-label dependencies. Evaluated using five-fold cross-validation and trained with an Asymmetric-Graph-Consistency loss, SRCWDNet achieves accuracies of 98.93% and 96.53%, with corresponding F1-scores of 0.9365 and 0.8723 on the MLRSNet and China-MAS50k datasets, respectively, outperforming CNN, transformer, and lightweight baselines. These findings demonstrate that jointly modeling visual attention and adaptive label correlations can substantially improve multi-label remote sensing scene classification, particularly under long-tailed class distributions.

##### **Self-Upgrading Embodied Agents**
2611.31117v1 by Jiajun Fan, Neil He, Qinglong Zheng, Changyi Yang, Tong Wei, Andy Mao, Zetian Li, Chaoran Cheng, Hui Ren, Ruihan Guo, Xianrui Zhong, Xizheng Yu, Jingyuan Li, Xi Zhu, Kaiqu Liang, Chang Xu, Zun Qian, Zidi Xiong, Yuezhu Zhao, Xu Huang, Ge Liu

An embodied agent built on a language model goes to work with the weights it was given and keeps them for life. To get better, it needs more training, or reflection, memory, search or prompts that cost every step and are lost when the task changes. Editing weights by reasoning, weight-space programming, could end this, but has stayed with models that only reason or relied on a far larger commercial model. We introduce the self-upgrading embodied agent, to our knowledge the first that learns from its own experience by upgrading its own weights on the job, between its tasks, with no training, no gradient, no reward model, no second model, no test-time scaling and no harness or prompt engineering. By day a 27B agent works in a household environment. At night it reads its record, finds many of its steps wasted on replies that reach the token limit before any action, traces the habit to one direction in its hidden states, and rewrites one tensor of its weights. An agent that rewrites itself must first never break itself, so it is its own examiner: it tests every change against its unchanged self on practice tasks and keeps only what holds. 2611.27100v1 by Chenruo Liu, Quan Zhang, Qi Lei

Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning exposes intermediate steps, but a correct answer does not establish that an LLM relies on the dependence expressed by those steps or reasons efficiently. We operationalize CoT faithfulness and efficiency within a common framework. Specifically, we represent a CoT as a semantic reasoning graph and decompose the model's predictive dependence along two complementary axes: dependence explained by semantic support versus additional reasoning history, and dependence allocated to answer-reaching versus off-path reasoning. This decomposition yields global and step-level measures of unfaithfulness and inefficiency.
