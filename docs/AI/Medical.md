##### **Retrieval-Augmented Correction for Generalist Medical Image Segmentation**
2611.23189v1 by Vincenzo Marcianò, Xiaoming Zhang, Michela Antonelli, Sebastien Ourselin, Maria A Zuluaga

Automated segmentation is routinely used as the first step in quantitative image-based clinical workflows, where predicted masks are subsequently reviewed and corrected by human operators. Automated correction of the residual structural errors is currently addressed one anatomy at a time, so the number of trained correctors scales with the number of segmentation targets. We present GRACE, a retrieval-augmented refiner that edits an input mask under context drawn from a shared knowledge base of verified prior cases. The refiner is trained once on a multi-organ corpus spanning compact organs, cavities, branching vessels, and tumour lesions. In the held-out dataset-transfer evaluation, three held-out datasets query a fixed knowledge base built from training data only. A separate leave-one-anatomy-out experiment tests parameter-free index extension: verified cases of an unseen solid organ enter the knowledge base, and the refiner stays frozen. A light shape-aware projection head on a frozen multimodal encoder maps embedding similarity to the shape agreement of the masks, so the knowledge base returns cases that the refiner can use as a shape prior. We evaluate GRACE on 18 public datasets spanning CT, MRI and ultrasound, using nine refinement configurations and seven upstream segmentors that span weak, mid, and strong operating regimes. We report each change with the DSC of its input mask. On the weak tier, the input DSC is 0.386 and GRACE adds up to +0.35 DSC. On a strong segmentor, the input DSC is 0.932 and the mean change is small. Transfer to an imaging modality that is absent from training and from the index is partial and depends on the anatomy, and remains an open research direction. Our results suggest retrieval-conditioned refinement is a practical alternative to anatomy-specific correction models. Code: https://github.com/robustml-eurecom/grace

##### **PeriaTrace: Tooth-Level Grounding Diagnosis and Patient-Facing Explanations from Periapical Radiographs**
2611.23012v1 by Zhenhuan Zhou, Yuchen Zhang, Jingbo Zhu, Shiqing Ma, Yanlin Wu, Tao Li

Periapical radiographs (PRs) are central to endodontic diagnosis, but interpreting them and preparing reports are time-consuming. Technical language can limit patients' understanding, while existing automated methods often lack tooth-level links between findings and image evidence. We present PeriaTrace, a three-stage framework integrating tooth-level diagnosis, visually grounded report generation, and patient-friendly explanations and care recommendations. PeriaTrace was developed and evaluated using 12,877 PRs from an internal dataset and two external clinical cohorts. In internal evaluation, it achieved a macro Dice coefficient of 85.35%, BLEU-4 of 0.6504, an F1 score of 94.37% for joint tooth localization and Fédération Dentaire Internationale (FDI) identification, and a grounding-tag F1 score of 77.93%. In the two external cohorts, senior endodontists considered 91.16% and 84.52% of reports suitable for retention or revision without complete rewriting. Generation averaged 4.41 seconds per PR, several tens of times faster than manual drafting. Among 196 survey participants without medical backgrounds, 96.4% preferred reports with plain-language explanations and care recommendations, and 84.7% found visual localization cues helpful. PeriaTrace may enable faster, evidence-traceable reporting while supporting clinical review and patient communication. (Abridged version)

##### **Large Language Models Have Not Yet Transformed Science**
2611.22975v1 by Ron Bekkerman, Nichole Li, Bahareh Kargar, Mark Somers

The emergence of large language models (LLMs) in late 2022 has raised questions about the future of science, but assessing their impact requires understanding how scientific practices were already evolving. We analyze 2.5 million full-text papers across five disciplines from 2015 to 2025, tracing trends over a broad spectrum of lexical, structural, epistemological, and social dimensions.
