##### **TeamGym: A Gym for Team Decision-Making by AI Agents with Distributed Information**
2610.37571v1 by Chanwoo Park, Jaywoong Jeong, Zachary Wojtowicz, Yubin Kim, Taekyun Lee, Kaiqing Zhang, Asuman Ozdaglar

Many real-world decisions require teams to integrate information distributed across participants. Yet AI agents are still primarily trained and benchmarked in isolation. We introduce TeamGym, an environment for evaluating and training language model-based agents on team decision-making problems constructed from real-world legal, finance, medical, and software engineering corpora. TeamGym introduces a method to identify a minimal set of facts, each element of which is necessary and all of which are collectively sufficient for a correct final decision, which we call pivotal information. This enables TeamGym to both control the distribution of pivotal information and track how well agents identify and integrate pivotal information when making team decisions. Examining 8 language models on 12 source corpora, we find that current AI agents often fail to effectively share information: Distributing pivotal information across participants lowers success rate by 22.9 percentage points on average relative to giving the same information to a single model (a communication gap). We also document that participants do not effectively retrieve decision-relevant information on their own: A team's success rate is lowered by a further 19.9 points when participants must identify and extract pivotal information from source material rather than receiving it directly (a retrieval gap). To help remedy these deficits, we use TeamGym to train policies through reinforcement learning on team decision loss. Across two policy-training experiments, we find that training improves final-decision quality and induces emergent coordination in participation, speaker selection, and communication, even though the reward evaluates only the final decision.

##### **When uncertainty quantification fails: structural vulnerabilities and corrections for selective prediction under clinical class imbalance**
2610.37561v1 by Walter Endrizzi, Flavio Ragni, Raffaele Marchesi, Stefano Bovo, Monica Moroni, Giuseppe Jurman, Venet Osmani

Background. Selective prediction systems improve the safety of clinical artificial intelligence by deferring ambiguous cases to human experts while automating high-confidence decisions. However, this safety mechanism relies entirely on the premise that uncertainty scores accurately reflect clinical ambiguity. In prognostic tasks characterized by severe class imbalance, where the clinical outcome of interest is a minority, this assumption breaks down. Standard uncertainty measures cease to capture clinically aligned ambiguity; instead, they become a proxy for class membership, systematically penalizing minority patients regardless of their individual predictive clarity.
  Results. Across 56 synthetic conditions and three neurodegenerative cohorts spanning severe class imbalance to near-balanced conditions (multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's Disease, Alzheimer's Disease), our framework consistently maintained bounded, balanced risk across trajectories. In contrast, global metrics either collapsed minority Sensitivity or protected the minority at the expense of majority patients. Applying CCR with standard entropy fails to protect the minority because it breaks within-class monotonicity when tau=0.5. Combining CCR with threshold-aware metrics restores strict monotonicity, correctly identifies ambiguous patients, and safely mitigates minority exclusion.
  Conclusions. Under clinical class imbalance, standard uncertainty quantification measures dataset sparsity rather than clinical ambiguity, creating a systemic illusion of safety. This bias is a correctable structural flaw. Clinical pipelines deploying selective prediction under imbalance should adopt threshold-aligned uncertainty measures and class-stratified evaluation. This reduces class-specific risk imbalance, supporting safer selective prediction under the evaluated conditions without silently excluding the most vulnerable patients.

##### **M4Bench: Evaluating Procedural Specification for Clinical EHR Derivation Agents**
2610.37459v1 by Hannes Ill, Rafi Al Attrach, Rajna Fani, Ahram Han, Ryohei Yamamoto, Marlene Louisa Moerig, Dukyong Yoon, Leo Anthony Celi

Clinical research over electronic health records depends on derived variables whose definitions sit outside the schema, in item codes, time windows, missingness rules, and output-grain choices that change cohorts and downstream conclusions.
