##### **VaDar: Implicit Goal Conditioning via Value Disaggregation**
2610.26629v1 by Shashwat Saxena, Mehul Goel, Sreyas Venkataraman, Sarvesh Patil, Max Simchowitz

Long-horizon tasks are important for modern AI and robotics, yet pose a challenge for reinforcement learning. Historically, a popular approach has been to decompose a task into subgoals, and learn a subgoal-conditioned policy to execute each subtask in sequence.
  We introduce an alternative called Value Disaggregation (VaDar), which trains a single subgoal-independent policy to maximize a linear combination of per-subgoal critic values. Because subgoal information is needed only at training-time, VaDar removes the need for possibly costly or cumbersome subgoal-selection during policy deployment. Moreover, through careful experiments, we show that VaDar (1) is always competitive with, and frequently outperforms, natural baselines like goal-conditioning and (2) succeeds on tasks with non-sequential goal structure and multiple optimization objectives. In particular, VaDar is the first algorithm to solve challenging, long-horizon tasks in the RoboCasa suite, even when starting from a policy pretrained from behavior cloning with near-zero initial task success. Taken together, VaDar's successes suggest that subgoal information benefits actor-critic reinforcement learning by enhancing the efficacy of critic learning, whereas policy goal-conditioning is often unnecessary.

##### **Autonomous intelligence for mining productivity: a multi-agent AI approach to operational value creation**
2610.26369v1 by N Guerin, A Dehoux, M Nakhla

Integrated Remote Operation Centres (IROCs) are emerging as the architectural frontier of Industry 4.0 for capital-intensive sectors such as mining, oil and gas, and energy. Yet two intertwined gaps constrain their evolution towards full autonomy. First, no integrated agentic architecture has been proposed for mining IROCs that orchestrates Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition systems, digital twins, predictive analytics and human-machine interfaces under safety-critical constraints. Second, no evaluation framework jointly addresses the real-time, Health-Safety-Environment, regulatory-auditability and cost-per-decision requirements that govern industrial agentic deployments. This paper makes four contributions. It synthesises industry needs and quantified gains documented in analogous industries that motivate an agentic approach to mining IROCs. It introduces EI2ROC, a three-tier multi-agent architecture combining a Large Language Model orchestrator with specialised monitoring and production-capacityprediction agents communicating through a Model Context Protocol-style tool interface. It provides a critical review of agentic evaluation along six orthogonal dimensions and identifies the industrial blind spot of current benchmarks. Finally, it proposes a multi-dimensional benchmarking framework for industrial agentic AI comprising scenario library, harness, metrics and humancalibration protocol. The paper states explicitly which components are implemented in the current prototype, which belong to the proposed reference design and which remain future work, and reports preliminary cost and latency observations from an operating site. Together, EI2ROC and the benchmarking framework constitute a methodological blueprint transferable across CAPEX intensive industries.

##### **Fairness or Fallacy? Mapping the Landscape of AI Bias in Disease Detection**
2610.26215v1 by Ghulam Mudassir, Hameed Ullah Khan, Ahmad Sami Al-Shamayleh, Muhammad Faizan Khan, Muhammad Muqtasid, Moamin Abughazala, Adnan Akhunzada

Artificial intelligence (AI) has several transformative effects on healthcare, like to facilitate clinicians in disease detection and to manage patient care with enhanced precision. Due to the increasing complexity of medical data and the growing volume of diagnostic images, the substantial computational power needed to process such information creates both opportunities and challenges. To accomplish this objective, numerous methodologies have been proposed to integrate AI into disease detection systems. All these methodologies consider different aspects of clinical diagnosis, considering key parameters such as the type of AI model employed, the nature of input data, and the population demographics being analysed. 2610.15884v2 by You Liu, Yue Liu, Quanchao Lu

False retry permission (FRP) occurs when an agent retries a timed-out tool call without support from the visible contract. RetryBench separates this error from realized recovery. Successful protected recovery does not imply reliable rejection of unsupported retries. In a 720-response key control, valid keys usually enabled safe progress, but offered changed keys elicited unsupported retries in Sol (1/80), Terra (55/80), and Luna (60/80). A later 240-response control removed the candidate and required models to supply a key. Luna still made 8/80 unsupported dispatches; Sol and Terra made none in 80 responses each. Accepting a displayed bad key therefore cannot explain all observed errors, although changed output requirements and conditions preclude a pure display-effect comparison. Removing the weak-contract label increased retries in Sol and Terra's coded-action conditions in a six-endpoint study. A separate 1,536-response canonical-action block also found a positive Terra contrast (115/128 versus 124/128 retries), concentrated in two task/order conditions; the other five contrasts remained unresolved after multiplicity correction. An explicit-contract cue control elicited deferral in all 762 returned responses, leaving recovery unresolved. These results support reporting permission and recovery together across declared interface variants. The evidence covers four purposive tasks, fixed GPT endpoints, and one recovery decision; it identifies neither internal mechanisms nor episode-level safety.

2610.15351v1 by Abdur Rahman, A. A. M. Muzahid, Istiak Ahmed, Dipto Biswas, Hua Han, Yujin Zhang, Ferdous Sohel

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) diagnosis remains challenging due to heterogeneous symptoms and reliance on clinical assessment, underscoring the need for objective, data-driven approaches that leverage patient-generated physiological signals. We propose Siam-ALS, an explainable Siamese deep learning framework that jointly models synchronized electroencephalography (EEG) and eye-tracking (ET) signals. EEG captures ALS-related changes in brain activity, while ET provides information about oculomotor control, allowing the model to learn complementary patterns associated with ALS. The framework employs two modality-specific encoders: a convolutional EEG encoder that captures spatiotemporal neural patterns and an MLP-based ET encoder that models oculomotor dynamics. These modality-specific features are processed through weight-sharing Siamese branches and fused into a compact 64-dimensional joint embedding. A lightweight classifier is used to predict ALS, while pairwise metric learning helps the model learn more effective patterns from the small, diverse ALS datasets. To enhance interpretability, six XAI methods were evaluated in the learned embedding space, with SHAP achieving the highest, though statistically comparable, fidelity in identifying important EEG and ET patterns that contribute to ALS predictions. Overall, Siam-ALS provides an accurate, generalizable, and explainable framework for multimodal ALS detection.

##### **ClinEdge: An Expert-Graded Benchmark of Real Patient Edge Cases Where Frontier Multimodal Models Fail**
2610.15239v1 by Anurag Sharma, Aditya Sharma, Sapna Baalwan

Most benchmarks for large language models in medicine are built from licensing examinations and theoretical questions. The few that draw on real clinical data do so only in part, and rarely require a model to read a patient's own imaging. ClinEdge addresses this gap with 32 real, de-identified patient cases from active clinical practice, each evaluated across seven frontier systems for a total of 224 expert-graded evaluations. Each case pairs primary imaging with a short vignette, contains a feature known to defeat pattern-matched reasoning, and is scored against the patient's documented clinical course. First, a senior orthopaedic surgeon graded all 224 responses on a weighted rubric and a pass-or-fail verdict. All seven production systems were evaluated through their public interfaces, and all 224 conversations are preserved as replayable links.

They trust a misleading history over correct imaging. Their answers vary across systems and runs, and change with question format. Failing answers were delivered fluently and without any expressed uncertainty. Four cases are released in full; the remaining 28 are held out, and we invite frontier AI labs and healthcare AI companies to evaluate their models against them (research@futrbridge.com)

##### **TurnCost: Turns, Not Observations: Cache-Aware Loop Engineering for Coding Agents**
2610.15093v1 by Pramod Kumar, Raj Kumar Pandey

AI coding agents operate in iterative loops: they modify code, run tools, inspect the results, and decide what to do next. We call the design of this process loop engineering, which includes context management, model selection, compaction, and termination. Existing efficiency work mainly compresses tool observations or routes selected turns to cheaper models, but the practical opportunity for these optimizations remains unclear. We analyze 600 publicly released coding-agent trajectories spanning two agent scaffolds and two language models. Median observations contain only 118-286 tokens, while the large build logs commonly used to motivate compression occur near the ninety-ninth percentile. Moreover, 98.6% of input tokens are served from a prefix cache, so flat input pricing overestimates loop cost by roughly 7x on average under near-ideal caching.
  We derive and empirically validate a closed-form break-even condition for model delegation. Delegation increases cost in half of the evaluated settings, near the predicted boundary. More broadly, turn count explains 84% of the variance in log trajectory cost, compared with 4% for mean observation size; the most expensive trajectories have the most turns despite having the smallest observations. These associations suggest that observation-focused methods target a secondary cost factor, and identify unnecessary turns as the larger and largely unexamined opportunity, though we do not establish that turns can be removed while holding task success fixed. We release TurnCost, a tool for re-scoring trajectory corpora under alternative caching, pricing, compression, and routing policies.
