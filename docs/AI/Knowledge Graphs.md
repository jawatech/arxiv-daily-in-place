##### **The End of Consensus Research?**
2610.30114v1 by Clément Burgelin, Rachid Guerraoui, Zicong Hong, Yinan Hu, Anne-Marie Kermarrec, Rafael Pires, Rasmus Moorits Veski, Martijn de Vos

Consensus is a fundamental problem in distributed computing and at the core of state machine replication (SMR). Despite decades of active human research, discovering practical low-latency solutions to the problem is still open, as it requires combining protocol knowledge, correctness reasoning, implementation, and experimental evaluation. We report on how AI helped us discover A-greement, a new consensus protocol for geo-replicated state machines that significantly outperforms state-of-the-art solutions. Averaged across six deployments with 5 or 9 replicas, A-greement reduces the mean latency of a geo-distributed key-value store over the fastest baseline by 12.2% and 33.3% on write-heavy and read-heavy workloads, respectively, and matches the fastest baseline on read-only workloads. A-greement combines lease-authorized local reads, direct client learning for leader-ordered writes, and a fast fixed-quorum per-key write path, selected according to network topology and workload. At the heart of our discovery lies AI-greement, an agent harness for the "autonomous" discovery, implementation, experimentation, and verification of candidate protocols. AI-greement integrates (1) a concept-oriented protocol knowledge base, (2) a modular implementation skeleton, and (3) a Lean-based verification framework with reusable safety definitions, reference specifications, and proofs. AI-greement is of independent interest: its Lean formalization uncovers several previously unreported vulnerabilities in existing consensus protocols, and it provides guidelines on how domain-specific harnesses can turn general-purpose coding agents into effective tools for (distributed) computing research. Yet we argue that human consensus experts remain essential before, during, and after the search: agents reshape the researcher's role rather than end consensus research.

##### **Did the Problem Actually Get Harder? Testing LLMs on Paired P and NP-Complete Problems Hardened by Adversarial Multi-Agent Rewriting**
2610.30103v1 by Nurul Labib Sayeedi, Md. Faiyaz Abdullah Sayeedi, Zabir Al Nazi, Mahbub E Sobhani, Shubhashis Roy Dipta

Many NP-complete (NPC) problems have a polynomial-time (P) twin that differs in a single structural detail, such as 3- versus 2-colouring a graph. A model that reasons rather than recalls should notice which side of this boundary a problem falls on, yet existing benchmarks either fix the wording of each task or ignore its complexity class, so they cannot separate the two. We introduce P2NPCBench, a benchmark of 12 problem families in which every NPC problem is paired with a P twin posed over the same surface. To remove the textbook cues that enable recall, we build it with AgentGAN, an adversarial multi-agent framework in which a generator rewrites each statement until a panel of frozen code models fails, while an executable validity gate keeps every rewrite answer- and class-preserving. The result is 2,114 hardened statements with hand-verified reference human solvers. Evaluating 12 proprietary and open-source models, we find that every model scores lower on NPC statements than on their P twins (35.4% vs. 63.8% pass@1), and that 95.1% of model-family pairs fail more often on the NPC side. A tenfold time limit raises NPC accuracy only from 28.5% to 31.9%, so failures stem from wrong or crashing programs rather than slow ones. Hardening also narrows the class gap from 24.8 to 14.6 points, showing that part of the apparent gap reflects familiarity with textbook phrasing rather than complexity class.

##### **Parallel AI Agent Collaboration as Graph-Structured Distributed Block Optimization**
2610.30074v1 by Hanmin Li, Yury Demidovich, Peter Richtárik

Parallel AI agents can produce useful local edits yet degrade a shared solution when their edits are combined. We model this coordination problem as discrete block optimization with an exactly evaluable objective and a known dependency graph. Independent sets certify compatible edits whose verified gains, measured against the same current solution, add exactly. A coordinator constructs a safe compatible candidate and can also evaluate interacting edits to capture favorable interactions. We prove that an optimal fractional coloring yields a merger that preserves a $1/χ_f(G)$ fraction of the total verified local improvement, where $χ_f(G)$ is the graph's fractional chromatic number. This guarantee is tight among mergers that select a subset of the same proposals. Under conditional local-improvement guarantees and a block error-bound condition, repeated collaboration converges geometrically in the objective gap.
