##### **Pixel: A Co-Designed Full-Stack Robot System for Learning General-Purpose Mobile Manipulation**
2611.03648v1 by Tony Tao, Jason Jingzhou Liu, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, Deepak Pathak

General-purpose mobile manipulation requires a combination of capable robot hardware, scalable demonstration collection, expressive whole-body control, and effective policy learning. Yet these components are often developed independently, creating mismatches that limit the capabilities of the complete system. We present Pixel, a co-designed full-stack system for learning general-purpose mobile manipulation. Pixel combines a low-cost wheeled humanoid with human-aligned kinematics and compliant grippers, multiple teleoperation and robot-free human data-collection interfaces, a unified whole-body controller, and a common policy learning pipeline across human and robot demonstrations. A hierarchical learning stack further enables language-conditioned visuomotor control and long-horizon task execution. We evaluate the system across hardware, data collection, and policy learning, and demonstrate mobile-manipulation behaviors requiring dexterity, active perception, navigation, and whole-body coordination. We hope that Pixel provides an integrated and extensible foundation for studying how robot hardware, data, control, and learning can be designed together for robot learning. ##### **From Natural Language to Exact Cover: A Neuro-Symbolic Approach to Zebra Puzzles**
2611.03641v1 by Paulius Skaisgiris, Thomas Pammer, Veronika Semmelrock, Mykyta Ielanskyi, Maximilian Heisinger, Erich Kobler

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation has substantially improved the performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) on complex reasoning tasks, including code generation, data analysis, and exam-style question answering. Despite these advances, purely neural LLMs continue to struggle with elementary logical reasoning problems and lack the determinism, soundness, and reliability characteristic of symbolic reasoning systems. Conversely, classical symbolic methods such as SAT solving and exact cover guarantee correctness and completeness, but require problems to be expressed in highly specialized formal encodings, limiting their applicability to natural language inputs. In this work, we present a synergistic neuro-symbolic framework that bridges this gap by combining neural semantic parsing with deterministic exact cover reasoning. Given a Zebra-style logic puzzle in natural language, an LLM translates entities and constraints into structured tool calls that construct an exact cover instance. A symbolic solver then computes the solution and provides feedback to iteratively refine invalid, under- or overconstrained encodings, exploiting the uniqueness property of Zebra puzzles. We conduct a comprehensive empirical evaluation across multiple parameter scales, post-training paradigms, and LLM families. The results demonstrate that our hybrid approach consistently outperforms strong plain neural baselines, including CoT prompting, as well as recent neuro-symbolic methods.

##### **D&A: Decoupling Description and Assessment Enables VLMs as Zero-Shot Robotic Reward Models**
2611.03622v1 by Marius Memmel, Shawn Yin, Urvi Rutia, Angad Wadhwa, Eldon Li, Gavin Angeloff, Ankit Goyal, Jesse Zhang, Dieter Fox, Fabio Ramos, Anqi Li, Abhishek Gupta

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) hold promise for zero-shot video task-progress estimation in robot learning, yet existing frameworks frequently suffer from confirmation bias, hallucinating progress in failed executions due to implicit success assumptions or relative temporal ranking designs. Naive prompting of frontier models collapses into a binary success detector that fails to capture nuanced, non-linear progress and failures. While post-training approaches can mitigate this through explicit training, they demand expensive real-world robotics data and require computationally intensive post-training for every individual base model. To resolve these limitations, we introduce D&A (Describe & Assess): our approach decouples progress estimation into an objective, task-grounded textual description of scene dynamics followed by an assessment phase using explicit rubrics. By compelling the VLM to synthesize an explicit, text-based definition of absolute success before scoring, the framework anchors evaluations to an external, global criterion rather than the video's internal timeline.
