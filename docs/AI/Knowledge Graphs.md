##### **Agentic AI for Discovering Fault-Tolerant Quantum Circuits**
2611.18504v1 by Aritra Lahiri, Florian Marquardt

Scaling quantum computing toward practical quantum advantage is currently limited by the efficiency of circuits that operate on information encoded by quantum error-correcting codes. The full stack of quantum computing encompasses several tasks: preparation of logical code states, implementation of logical gates, logical routines on encoded qubits, etc. Yet, these circuits are largely designed by hand for individual cases, with some recent works incorporating machine learning for certain aspects. We formulate this problem as agentic evolutionary program synthesis, wherein a large language model, leveraging its pretraining knowledge and tool-use proficiency, and guided by a circuit verifier, evolves a program that defines a Clifford circuit for a wide range of curated tasks such as logical state preparation, logical gate design, and logical routines such as code-switching, code growth, etc. Our approach consistently finds fault-tolerant circuits for all of these tasks, each verified by an exhaustive check over faults.

##### **Black-Box Uncertainty Quantification for Large Language Models via Ensemble-of-Ensembles**
2611.18453v1 by Wang Ma, Debarun Bhattacharjya, Junkyu Lee, Nhan H Pham, Harsha Kokel, Qiang Ji

Reliable use of large language models (LLMs) requires uncertainty estimates that separate ambiguity intrinsic to a prompt from genuine knowledge gaps. Bayesian and deep-ensemble methods deliver this aleatoric/epistemic decomposition in principle, but are computationally prohibitive at LLM scale and require white-box access. Existing black-box methods, in contrast, summarize to a single scalar that cannot make the distinction. We introduce a two-level ensemble that bridges this gap. Stochastic decoding of a fixed prompt forms the inner ensemble; meaning-preserving semantic perturbations of the prompt form the outer ensemble. Variability is measured in a continuous embedding space, and the law of total covariance gives an exact within-/between-perturbation decomposition to estimate aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty (AU/EU); it requires only black-box sampling access. On top of this estimator, we contribute three theoretical results that ground the framework: (i) a finite-sample bias identity for nested Monte Carlo with a closed-form bias-corrected epistemic estimator; (ii) a Bayesian bridge bounding the gap between perturbation-population AU/EU and Bayesian targets; and (iii) a separation result identifying a class of failure modes that any consistency-only black-box method provably misses while perturbation-based EU detects. Across five short QA benchmarks and four instruction-tuned LLMs spanning two families and two scales, our estimator is competitive with strong white-box baselines, and at a matched generation budget it matches leading black-box methods on error ranking while decomposing AU and EU; controlled paired interventions show that AU and EU respond selectively to distinct, actionable sources of failure: prompt ambiguity and model knowledge gaps; and a long-form pilot on the NQ long-answer split shows the framework extends to claim-level factuality without retraining.

##### **LECAT: Measuring LLM Category Learning from Pretraining Data**
2611.18419v1 by Jimin Mun, Maarten Sap, Yanai Elazar

Humans know that airplanes are fast, that this extends to red airplanes, but that it does not hold for broken airplanes. How do LLMs acquire category knowledge that humans learn and apply effortlessly? We introduce LeCat, a two-part framework to analyze LLM's Learning of Category knowledge. First, we use LeCat to evaluate models on their ability to learn category knowledge, distinguishing between three cases: memorization of in-domain category knowledge, generalization to unseen instances, and ability to flexibly use knowledge in appropriate contexts. Then, we use LeCat to trace how this knowledge can be learned in pretraining from text that may encode category knowledge in various ways, such as verbatim repetition or exceptions, which we refer to as forms of "knowledge expression". We first assess open-source pretrained models using 199 curated category-feature pairs and analyze their training data. Our findings suggest that strengthening context and category-feature co-occurrence likelihood (PMI) improve learning for larger models and verbatim rule-like statements diminish flexible category learning. To systematically isolate these effects, we then train a 68M model from scratch with precise control over the categorical information each sees in their training data, confirming that verbatim repetition reduces flexible learning but mixture of diverse knowledge expressions lead to more robust category learning. Together, our findings highlight that while contextual flexibility is persistently difficult for models, how category knowledge is expressed in pretraining greatly impacts models' acquisition of robust category knowledge.

##### **Vision-Language-Action Models: A Life-Cycle Survey of Design, Training and Deployment**
2611.18372v1 by Suvarna Kadam, Soran Parsa

Vision-language-action (VLA) models adapt web-pretrained vision-language models to output robot actions, so that web knowledge carries over to physical control. In under 4 years the field has moved from RT-2, the first VLA, to open models that fine-tune on a single consumer GPU and to proprietary generalists that clean unseen homes.
