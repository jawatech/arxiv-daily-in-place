##### **Selective Growing Q-Networks (SGQN): An Analysis of Adaptive Action Grids in Decoupled Q-Learning**
2610.20463v1 by Parham Nooranbakht, Seyed Mahdi Basiri Azad, Joschka Bödecker

Value-based Q-learning for continuous control can discretise the action space, but the resolution of the discretisation trades early exploration against final precision. Growing Q-Networks (GQN) grow the resolution uniformly from coarse to fine. We introduce Selective Growing Q-Networks (SGQN), which keeps decoupled Q-learning and replaces uniform growth with an adaptive action grid: bins are added next to bins with high estimated Q-value, bins with low Q-value are pruned, and actions are chosen epsilon-greedily. On eight DeepMind Control Suite (DMControl) tasks with 2 to 12 action dimensions, SGQN has the highest mean final return among the compared methods on four, is within 1.1% of the best method on three more, and is 3.4% below Bang-Bang MPO on Walker Run, using on average 7.5 of 9 available bins per dimension. We then test whether the adaptive grid explains these results, and find that it does not. Growth activates every candidate bin and behaves like uniform growth, and removing pruning changes the return by at most 1.1%. Two other choices matter: starting from a coarse grid, since starting with all 9 bins produces collapsed seeds, and epsilon-greedy selection, since an annealed softmax lowers the return by 23% to 100%. SGQN is insensitive to its growth and pruning hyperparameters. It also differs from the GQN and DecQN baselines in other respects, notably one gradient update per environment step instead of one per eight, which we did not control for, so we do not attribute its advantage to a single component. We present SGQN as a competitive value-based method and as an analysis of which ingredients of a growing action grid matter.

##### **From Training to Escalation: Forensic Analysis and Local Recreation of RL/RLHF-Induced Privilege Escalation in AI Agents (CVE-2026-65616)**
2610.20437v1 by Alicia Colmenero-Fernández

Mid-2026 reporting on autonomous AI evaluation incidents claimed that frontier large language model (LLM) agents escaped server containment through emergent coordination and cloud infrastructure attacks. This paper contextualizes those claims through forensic analysis and containerized recreation of both the Highly Persistent Internal Model (HPIM) training-case conditions and the Phase One privilege escalation targeting internal package repositories during OpenAI's ExploitGym evaluations. We recreate the configuration of the OpenAI-provided training example and reproduce the cryptographic verification bypass (CVE-2026-65616) in isolated repository servers, where misconfigured systems enabled token forgery and privilege escalation through basic HTTP requests, Python, and JavaScript. Crucially, the training-example conditions and the Phase One (and later) misconfigurations share the same exploitable patterns. Our analysis explains how Zero Trust Architecture principles neutralize this shared attack vector. Contemporary AI risks derive directly from training and deployment conditions in ambivalent learning environments lacking adequate containment measures.

##### **Robust Counterfactuals for Markov Decision Processes under Statistical and Causal Uncertainties**
2610.20210v1 by Jessica Lally, Milad Kazemi, Nicola Paoletti

Counterfactual reasoning (asking what would have happened, had a decision been different) is a powerful tool for identifying causes of failure and explaining the behaviour of AI systems in sequential decision-making tasks. In Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), two sources of uncertainty complicate counterfactual inference: statistical uncertainty in the MDP transitions when the MDP is estimated from trajectory data, and uncertainty about the unknown causal model, as there are multiple structural causal models (SCMs) compatible with each MDP. Existing work addresses these two challenges in isolation.
