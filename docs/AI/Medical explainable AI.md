##### **LUCQUE: A lung cancer screening dataset from Québec for reliable AI evaluation**
2611.12817v1 by Johanna Topalis, Yannick Lemaréchal, Gabriel Couture, Hanife Ersay, Loran Nas, Balthasar Schachtner, Bastian Oliver Sabel, Michael Ingrisch, Philippe Després, Katharina Jeblick

BACKGROUND: AI-based tools are expected to play a key role in lung cancer screening (LCS). The evaluation of these tools often relied on private datasets or fragmented public datasets with inconsistent availability of clinical data and expert nodule annotations. This has hindered reproducibility, comparability, and independent assessment of AI tools. To address these gaps, we present LUCQUE, a novel, large, comprehensively annotated dataset derived from LDCT lung cancer screening in Québec, which is accessible for reliable AI evaluation.
  METHODS: LUCQUE was curated and is hosted on PARADIM, a digital infrastructure supporting DICOM data de-identification, annotation, long-term storage and analysis. LDCT scans and clinical information were combined to a curated collection and complemented by voxel-level, expert-derived pulmonary nodule and cyst segmentations, and Lung-RADS-based lesion characteristics. Key dataset characteristics and statistics were reported, including participant demographics, imaging parameters, radiological findings, and segmented lesion characteristics. Dataset analyses were conducted according to PARADIM's model-to-data approach, in which user code is executed on the platform such that data remains on premise.
  RESULTS: LUCQUE currently comprises 1,678 LDCT baseline studies from 1,678 participants. Most studies were classified as Lung-RADS 2. A total of 1,776 pulmonary nodules and 60 cysts were annotated, with a median (interquartile range) volume of $89\,(56,\,159)\,\mathrm{mm}^3$ and $1,384\,(589,\,3793)\,\mathrm{mm}^3$ respectively.
  CONCLUSIONS: Compared to publicly available LCS datasets, LUCQUE currently represents the largest accessible annotated real-world LCS dataset. Access to LUCQUE is provided via a model-to-data approach, enabling privacy-preserving, reproducible, and independent real-world evaluation of AI tools in LCS.

##### **LogCDM: Interpretable Log Anomaly Detection via Behavioral Graph Drift and Adaptive Signal Fusion**
2611.12775v1 by Suryansh Talukdar, Taha Sarwat, Jin Soung Yoo

Log-based anomaly detection is increasingly dominated by large pretrained models, from BERT-scale encoders to 8B-parameter language models, which require GPUs at inference time and are difficult to deploy on the monitoring nodes where logs are produced. We present LogCDM, a compact detector that scores each log session using three complementary signals: the next-event surprise of a small causal Transformer, the distance between a graph-attention embedding of the session's own transition graph and that of a normal reference graph, and a transition-drift statistic that we show is exactly a frequency-weighted chi-squared divergence between transition kernels. The divergence view yields an asymptotic null distribution, a length-standardized score, and a per-edge decomposition that explains each alarm. A sigmoid gate fuses the three signals; under a shared-covariance Gaussian model, this gate is the Bayes posterior, and its learned weights indicate which signal each dataset relies on. With 8.1-8.8M parameters and no GPU required at inference, LogCDM reaches F1 scores of 0.984 on HDFS, 0.870 on chronologically split BGL, and 0.850 on Thunderbird, within 0.013-0.12 of an 8.1B-parameter LLM detector evaluated under the same protocol and above all compact baselines on BGL. It scores a session in 5.5-10.5 ms on a CPU. Ablations show that the structural signals add 0.025-0.030 F1 on BGL and Thunderbird but almost nothing on HDFS, consistent with the learned fusion weights.

##### **Library of Hands: Fix the Hands, Not the World Model**
2611.12678v1 by Hasaan Ahmad, Qizhen Ying, Yuxuan Sun, Tianjin Huang, Guangming Wang

A world model predicts how a scene changes after an action, and a planner uses it to search for actions that reach a goal. If the camera moves or the controller changes, the same command moves the robot differently: when its commands are turned by 90 degrees, a frozen planner with the LeWM world model that succeeds in 77.5% of episodes succeeds in only 7.8%. Current fixes retrain the model, need training data, or forget a set-up when it returns. We propose Library of Hands (LoH), a training-free method that keeps the world model frozen and corrects only the map from commands to applied actions, which we call the hand.
