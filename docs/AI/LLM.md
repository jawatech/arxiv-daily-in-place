##### **Modeling and Understanding Neural Weights of Humanoid Policies with Diffusion**
2610.20606v1 by Huixuan Cheng, Bike Zhang, Shankar Sastry, Koushil Sreenath

Recent advances in robot learning have driven rapid progress in humanoid control, enabling increasingly agile behaviors in the real world. However, humanoid control networks are typically obtained through conventional training procedures, while comparatively little attention has been given to directly modeling the distribution of their learned weights and generating functional networks in weight space. In this work, we investigate whether high-performing humanoid control networks can be generatively modeled directly through their weights. We adopt a latent diffusion framework that couples a linear autoencoder with a Diffusion Transformer. The autoencoder compresses actor network parameters into a compact latent space, and the Diffusion Transformer models the distribution of these latents. Trained on networks collected from reinforcement learning for the Unitree G1 robot, our approach generates deployable networks that perform various motions in the real world, including walking, dancing, and spin kick. Beyond generation, we further study the learned weight space to better understand the structure captured by these humanoid control networks. We identify a structural principle in the first-layer weights that consistently reflects the robot's kinematic prior. Notably, this kinematic prior emerges under both Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL) training, suggesting that it is not specific to a particular optimization process but may instead reflect common knowledge associated with humanoid embodiment. Together, these results demonstrate the feasibility of generative modeling in humanoid network weight space, while providing initial insights into the information encoded by their learned weights. Additional results are available on our project website: https://humanoid-weight-diffusion.github.io/

##### **Co-Evolving Acting and Forecasting for Language Agents**
2610.20597v1 by Haofei Yu, Yixing Chen, Yining Zhao, Xuyan Ye, Yuxiang Liu, Zhigang Hua, Yan Xie, Shuang Yang, Jiaxuan You

Training language agents is bottlenecked by the high cost of long-horizon rollouts, yet standard agentic reinforcement learning (RL) learns only from a terminal scalar reward, leaving the supervision in intermediate steps largely unused. Prior work exploits this supervision through value targets that estimate per-step success probabilities or observation targets that fit environment response. Both are hard to construct well: (1) value targets come from the same single scalar signal for each step in the trajectory, and (2) observation targets, such as web pages and terminal logs, are long and noisy and reach only one step ahead. We propose \name, which adds a third kind of multi-step action targets. On successful on-policy trajectories, \name trains the policy backbone to generate the action sequences that follows each history. Each action is also supervised when forecast from the preceding histories, forming a temporal ensemble that the shared backbone absorbs during training, and this enables acting and forecasting to co-evolve. The policy still acts one step at a time at inference, with no overhead. Across five benchmarks (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, WebShop, $τ^2$-bench, and AppWorld), \name (1) achieves stronger aggregate performance than GRPO and RL baselines with predictive auxiliary targets across simulated environments, while improving overall performance on both realistic applications; (2) delivers up to $1.9\times$ and $2.0\times$ higher sample efficiency than ECHO and GRPO, respectively, on ScienceWorld; and (3) achieves these gains with a single policy backbone, without additional models, separate pretraining, or inference overhead. The codebase is available at https://github.com/ulab-uiuc/CoPE.

##### **Your Transformer Is Secretly Elastic**
2610.20588v1 by Walter Simoncini, Dheeraj Varghese, Gertjan J. Burghouts, Cees G. M. Snoek

Scaling up transformers has produced models with broad capabilities, deployed across heterogeneous hardware and fluctuating workloads. Yet, model families offer only a few fixed sizes (e.g., S, B, L), forcing users to commit to a model based on hardware and latency constraints. This rigidity is often suboptimal under varying query loads. We introduce a retraining-free method that elastifies pre-trained transformers into a single model that adapts on-the-fly across a continuum of computational budgets. They can therefore observe how uncertain the model is, but not what it is uncertain about. For the three open-weight models, cross-dataset transfer between HotpotQA and TriviaQA with the model held fixed improves mean ROC-AUC over transferred LOS-Net by 3.76 points.

##### **Safe-FCL: Continual Risk Awareness and Fine-Grained Safety Aggregation for Federated Continual Learning of LLMs**
2610.19968v1 by Shuaijie Ma, Yongkang Hu, Yu Cheng, Jiuan Zhou, Yi Xie, Yushuo Zhang, Zhaoxia Yin

Federated fine-tuning enables large language models (LLMs) to learn collaboratively from distributed client data, but malicious client updates can compromise global model safety. As federated tasks evolve, changing malicious update patterns challenge the ability of existing defenses to maintain reliable risk detection over time.
