##### **GLOBE: Evolving Structured Agentic Memory for E-commerce Product Optimization in Generative Search**
2610.23709v1 by Hyunseo Kim, Suil Choe, Sukkyoung So, Daeyeon Yi

Optimizing product listings for generative search requires language agents not only to rewrite effectively but also to continually self-improve by retaining successful strategies across diverse categories. However, existing approaches often treat structured listings as free-form text, leaving effective strategies implicit in prompts, search trajectories, or rewritten outputs rather than accumulating them as a reusable agentic memory. To address this limitation, we introduce GLOBE, an agentic framework that drives continual self-improvement by storing optimization knowledge in an evolving handbook of explicit, field-scoped behaviors. In each episode, an Editor applies relevant behaviors and receives rank-based feedback, which a Reflector converts into behavior updates. Crucially for dependable execution in real-world settings, a Moderator removes redundant proposals, a Curator commits accepted changes, and consistently harmful behaviors are deactivated. This cycle transforms episodic feedback into a persistent, field-scoped optimization memory. Experiments show that structured behaviors outperform monolithic prompt optimization, while feedback-driven evolution and moderation further improve effectiveness and reduce downside risk. The learned behaviors also remain effective on unseen product categories, suggesting that agentic memory evolution supports interpretable and transferable product optimization.

##### **GEMS: Molecular Structure Elucidation as Learned Search over Isomer Graphs**
2610.23617v1 by Utku Umur Acikalin, Yingheng Wang, Goncalo J. Gouveia, Tyler J. Schwertfeger, Frank C. Schroeder, Carla P. Gomes

Identifying small-molecule structures from molecular formulas and tandem mass spectra remains a bottleneck in untargeted metabolomics and natural-product discovery. Even when database retrieval or de novo prediction misses the exact structure, the resulting candidates can provide starting points for exploring nearby structures through a few bond edits. We introduce GEMS (Guided Editing of Molecular Structures), which refines candidates through learned 2-switch bond edits that preserve the molecular formula and each atom's total valence. GEMS uses MIST, a pretrained spectrum-to-fingerprint model, to predict fingerprint probabilities once per spectrum. A neural policy compares each candidate's fingerprint with these predictions, links present features to the atoms and bonds that generate them, and scores all valid edits in one forward pass. The policy is trained on shortest edit paths between structures, so it can be pretrained on PubChem isomers without spectra and then fine-tuned with spectrum-predicted fingerprints. At inference, GEMS searches from multiple initial candidates matching the supplied molecular formula and ranks candidates by fingerprint agreement and multi-start consensus. With the target structure excluded from PubChem initialization, three edit rounds achieve Hit@1/Hit@10 of 29.64%/50.81% on NPLIB1 and 30.22%/41.33% on MassSpecGym, exceeding published baselines under comparable settings. On NPLIB1, GEMS achieves higher identification accuracy in about 3.3 minutes end to end, compared with 26.2 hours for our FRIGID run. GEMS also improves identification from generated candidates, including those refined by FRIGID. Experiments with exact target fingerprints identify fingerprint prediction error as a bottleneck in target recovery and final ranking. Deeper search recovers additional targets without retraining, with larger gains from weaker initial candidates.

##### **From Trajectories to Differentiated Skills: Cognitive Differentiation for Self-Improving Agents**
2610.23599v1 by Yewei Zhao, Derong Xu, Wei Chen, Yin Xu, Peilun Zhou, Xiaohan Wang, Guojun Yin, Wei Lin, Mingjun Xiao

Large language model (LLM) agents tackle complex tasks through reasoning and interaction with external environments. Agent skills provide persistent, transferable capabilities that guide task planning, inform contextual decisions, support tool use, and enable executable procedures. Yet existing skill-evolution methods largely organize knowledge around skill artifacts, overlooking the functional heterogeneity within individual skills and its implications for knowledge maintenance and use. Inspired by cognitive science, we propose Cognitive Differentiation in Skill Evolution (CDSE), which evolves a unified skill through four functionally distinct yet coordinated components: Meta, Experience, Tool, and Script. CDSE extracts evidence-grounded semantic gradients from successful and failed trajectories, consolidates them by functional role, and applies component-specific updates and validation with cross-component coordination and adaptive invocation. An analysis of 500 top-ranked skill packages shows that 96.8% contain knowledge spanning at least two functional roles. Across six benchmarks, three agent models, and two execution harnesses, CDSE achieves the highest scores in all 36 settings, improving over the Base agent by 7.6 percentage points and the strongest competing method in each setting by 2.6 points on average. ##### **Amortizing Sequential Experimental Design in Reasoning LLMs**

Despite their extensive pretraining knowledge and strong reasoning capabilities, large language models (LLMs) struggle to adaptively gather information in experimental design tasks. We study how to amortize sequential Bayesian experimental design (BED) into an LLM by explicitly training the reasoning that guides action selection using information-gain rewards. Our approach specifies the experimental generative model independently of the LLM's own beliefs, allowing us to optimize expected information gain under a model of our choosing. ##### **ARIA: Runtime Inspection and Amendment for Multi-Agent Systems**
2610.20780v1 by Bolin Shen, Yushun Dong

In LLM-based multi-agent systems (MAS), a local mistake can propagate as other agents build on it, turning a correctable error into a failed task. Diagnosing completed runs or repeatedly re-executing them to search for corrections does not provide a learned policy for intervening while execution is still in progress. We present Agent Runtime Inspection and Amendment (ARIA), a framework for learning when and how to repair ongoing multi-agent executions. Our key intuition is to turn execution-verified repairs into reusable supervision, moving repair search into data construction and training so that a learned model can intervene at runtime without repeatedly testing alternatives. First, we collect over 24{,}000 successful and failed trajectories across four multi-agent frameworks, six benchmarks, and eight host models. Using verified repairs as training examples, we teach a compact model to recognize when intervention is needed and how to correct an ongoing run. The aim is to learn repair strategies that apply to related failures in new configurations, rather than merely reproduce individual corrections. Finally, the trained ARIA model demonstrates strong repair performance in both post-hoc and runtime settings, with generalization to unseen frameworks and host models. Humans interpret multimodal observations of the world and forecast how it will evolve.
