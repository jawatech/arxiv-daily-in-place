##### **Can Language Models Play the Wikipedia Game?**
2610.13948v1 by Alexander Hickey, Joanna Knight, James Bishop

Language models increasingly support or automate information retrieval from document collections too large to index. This task is difficult when it requires specialist linguistic or scientific knowledge. We used the Wikipedia Game to test language models on an analogous information retrieval task. In the game, players must reach a target article using only article links. We compared sentence embedding models and LLMs across five Wikipedia languages and five academic-literature datasets. We construct very large local graphs of Wikipedia pages and of paper citations using OpenAlex and provide code to build and navigate them. This allows the study of language models' navigation capabilities without relying on third-party APIs. We compared the models with human and TF-IDF benchmarks. The Wikipedia Game format enables robust comparisons across techniques, languages, and domains. No model significantly outperformed humans, although the best models (GPT-o3 mini, GPT-4o, QwQ:32B, and DeepSeek-R1:70B) achieved comparable success. Performance and rankings varied across languages and topics, with implications for practitioners utilising these techniques.

摘要：語言模型越來越多地支持或自動從過於龐大的文檔集合中檢索信息。當這項任務需要專業的語言或科學知識時，它變得更加困難。我們使用維基百科遊戲來測試語言模型在類似的信息檢索任務上的表現。在遊戲中，玩家必須僅通過文章鏈接來達到目標文章。我們比較了五種維基百科語言和五個學術文獻數據集中的句子嵌入模型和大型語言模型（LLMs）。我們使用 OpenAlex 構建了非常大的維基百科頁面和論文引用的本地圖，並提供了構建和導航它們的代碼。這使得研究語言模型的導航能力不再依賴於第三方 API。我們將這些模型與人類和 TF-IDF 基準進行了比較。維基百科遊戲格式使得在技術、語言和領域之間進行穩健的比較成為可能。沒有模型顯著超越人類，儘管最佳模型（GPT-o3 mini、GPT-4o、QwQ:32B 和 DeepSeek-R1:70B）達到了可比的成功。性能和排名在不同語言和主題之間有所變化，對於利用這些技術的從業者具有重要意義。

##### **When VLMs Destroy What They Know: Spatial Representations Fall Below Lexical Priors**
2610.13910v1 by Shubh Garg, Chen Shani

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) increasingly analyze and reason about images, yet it remains unclear whether their internal representations faithfully encode the structured relations required for such reasoning. We introduce EICS (Evaluation-Invariant Concept Stability), a metric for testing whether representations consistently encode concepts under controlled one-concept interventions, and CASP-VL (Causally controlled Spatial Perturbations for Vision-Language models), a benchmark of causally controlled spatial perturbations derived from Visual Genome scene graphs. Across multiple VLM architectures, we find that spatial relations are represented substantially less faithfully than lexical information, even when the same models score well above chance on compositional benchmarks.
