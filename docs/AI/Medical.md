##### **ECG for laboratory values:A comprehensive physiological and technical analysis**
2610.28507v1 by Walentin Palanchuk

Laboratory measurements are essential for clinical decision making but typically require invasive blood sampling. Recent research suggests that ECG recordings may contain information beyond cardiac rhythm and provide information related to laboratory values. This thesis investigates NT-proBNP prediction from ECG recordings and patient metadata using a multimodal deep learning framework, while considering the physiological plausibility of ECG-based prediction. It also compares a 1-lead configuration using lead II with the corresponding 12-lead configuration. Additional experiments on glucose, hemoglobin, and potassium are presented in the appendix.
  Regression, binary classification, and three-class classification experiments were conducted using MIMIC-IV, with the three-class task based on clinically established thresholds. The 12-lead configuration consistently achieved better performance. Regression reached a mean absolute error of 534.98 pg/mL for 1-lead and 514.27 pg/mL for 12-lead. Binary classification achieved AUROCs of 0.849 and 0.876, respectively, while three-class classification achieved macro-averaged AUROCs of 0.738 and 0.752.
  The results indicate that precise NT-proBNP regression remains challenging, whereas broader concentration ranges can be classified more effectively. The findings are consistent with the physiological hypothesis that ECG characteristics and NT-proBNP concentrations reflect shared cardiovascular processes. ECG-based NT-proBNP prediction may therefore provide complementary information, particularly for classifying broader concentration ranges. However, laboratory measurements remain necessary, and validation on independent datasets is required.

##### **Brain2Vec: A Self-Supervised 3D Foundation Model for Structural Brain MRI**
2610.28411v1 by Spiros Maggioros, Guray Erus, Gareth Harman, George Aidinis, Alexander Getka, Aristeidis Sotiras, Christos Davatzikos

Deep learning models have become essential tools in medical imaging, yet training them end-to-end for diverse tasks remains challenging due to limited labeled data and computational constraints. To address this, we introduce Brain2Vec, a foundation model pre-trained on 74,425 T1-weighted Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) scans pooled from multiple studies. Brain2Vec can serve both as a weight initialization backbone for fine-tuning downstream tasks and as a standalone feature extractor. We evaluated Brain2Vec in three tasks: downstream predictive performance, convergence speed, and embedding quality. Using Brain2Vec's pretrained weights as initialization and fine-tuning on labeled data for a brain age prediction task, results demonstrate improved final accuracy and faster convergence compared to random initialization. Additionally, its frozen embeddings outperform anatomically defined volumetric features on four of five downstream classification tasks and perform competitively with a state-of-the-art foundation model pre-trained on substantially more data. Lastly, we show that Brain2Vec encodes information primarily from a spatially localized and neuroanatomically meaningful set of brain regions, and that manipulating a subset of latent features produces monotonic, region-specific effects on the reconstructed anatomy. Brain2Vec code and trained models are available as an open-source package on https://github.com/CBICA/Brain2Vec. Users can apply Brain2Vec locally by installing the package, or directly through the NiChart cloud platform https://cloud.neuroimagingchart.com, where Brain2Vec features can be derived without requiring local installation or specialized infrastructure.

##### **Gated Prototype Transfer for Zero-Support Medical Vision Language Model**
2610.28350v1 by Sumaiya Fazal, Rizwan Qureshi, Laleh Behjat, Samira Ebrahimi Kahou

Realistic few-shot adaptation of medical vision-language models (VLMs) can leave low-prevalence classes without any labeled support. The training-free SS- Text+ solver remains operational in this setting, but its zero-support prototypes receive no visual adaptation and fall back to a purely textual class prototype. We in- troduce Gated Knowledge-Guided Prototype Transfer (GProtoT), a pre-repulsion module that transfers supported-class visual residuals to missing classes using VLM similarity and an ontology-based clinical distance. A validation-free evidence gate scales transfer based on semantic relevance and residual agreement, thereby limiting unsupported extrapolation.
