##### **MuonGS: Pretraining LLMs with Relaxed Weight-Norm Control**
2610.37647v1 by Athanasios Glentis, Dawei Li, Mingyi Hong

Weight decay and explicit weight-norm constraints improve optimizer performance in language model pretraining, yet a unified understanding of why such control helps and how much is needed remains incomplete. We analyze norm control in Muon over the entire training trajectory using the \emph{accumulated effective learning rate}, $C_T=\sum_{t<T}η_t/\|W_t\|_F$, where $η_t$ is the nominal learning rate and $η_t/\|W_t\|_F$ is the effective learning rate relative to the weight norm. For bounded update directions, accumulation over any training interval bounds relative weight displacement. We show that vanilla Muon can accumulate only logarithmically, potentially limiting exploration and helping explain its slower empirical progress, whereas MuonW and MuonH guarantee at least linear accumulation under the stated conditions. Going beyond bounded-norm control, we show that sufficiently slow norm growth guarantees faster accumulation than the logarithmic worst case, opening up less restrictive optimizer designs.
  Based on this insight, we propose MuonGS, which uses Gram--Schmidt (GS) projection to remove the radial component of each Muon update without fixing the weight norm. With bounded update directions and a constant nominal learning rate, we prove that the weight norm grows at most proportionally to $\sqrt{t}$ after $t$ steps. Consequently, MuonGS guarantees $C_T=Ω(\sqrt{T})$, with accumulation sustained even in later training. Further, for smooth scale-invariant objectives we establish its $O(T^{-1/2})$ stationarity guarantee. In pretraining experiments with 160M- and 400M-parameter dense models as well as a 135M active- and 1B total- parameter MoE model, MuonGS achieves lower validation perplexity than MuonH at long training horizons. In particular, for the dense model it achieves an estimated token-equivalent speedup up to about $15\%$ at matched validation loss to MuonH.

##### **TeamGym: A Gym for Team Decision-Making by AI Agents with Distributed Information**
2610.37571v1 by Chanwoo Park, Jaywoong Jeong, Zachary Wojtowicz, Yubin Kim, Taekyun Lee, Kaiqing Zhang, Asuman Ozdaglar

Many real-world decisions require teams to integrate information distributed across participants. Yet AI agents are still primarily trained and benchmarked in isolation. We introduce TeamGym, an environment for evaluating and training language model-based agents on team decision-making problems constructed from real-world legal, finance, medical, and software engineering corpora. TeamGym introduces a method to identify a minimal set of facts, each element of which is necessary and all of which are collectively sufficient for a correct final decision, which we call pivotal information. This enables TeamGym to both control the distribution of pivotal information and track how well agents identify and integrate pivotal information when making team decisions. Examining 8 language models on 12 source corpora, we find that current AI agents often fail to effectively share information: Distributing pivotal information across participants lowers success rate by 22.9 percentage points on average relative to giving the same information to a single model (a communication gap). We also document that participants do not effectively retrieve decision-relevant information on their own: A team's success rate is lowered by a further 19.9 points when participants must identify and extract pivotal information from source material rather than receiving it directly (a retrieval gap). To help remedy these deficits, we use TeamGym to train policies through reinforcement learning on team decision loss. Across two policy-training experiments, we find that training improves final-decision quality and induces emergent coordination in participation, speaker selection, and communication, even though the reward evaluates only the final decision.

##### **M4Bench: Evaluating Procedural Specification for Clinical EHR Derivation Agents**
2610.37459v1 by Hannes Ill, Rafi Al Attrach, Rajna Fani, Ahram Han, Ryohei Yamamoto, Marlene Louisa Moerig, Dukyong Yoon, Leo Anthony Celi

Clinical research over electronic health records depends on derived variables whose definitions sit outside the schema, in item codes, time windows, missingness rules, and output-grain choices that change cohorts and downstream conclusions. We introduce M4Bench, 28 clinical derivation tasks across 15 task families on MIMIC-IV and eICU, to test whether AI coding agents can reproduce such derivations and how clinician-reviewed agent skills change their results. Its controls decompose the value of skills into specification completeness, delivery format, and schema familiarity. In a Docker-isolated Codex campaign (GPT 5.4 Mini and GPT 5.5), skills raise reward by 0.136 over the no-skill baseline, with 26 of 28 tasks improving.
