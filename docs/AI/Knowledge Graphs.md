##### **Scalable Conversational Assessment: Chat-Based Exams with a Two-Agent LLM Pipeline**
2611.17220v1 by Geoffrey Challen, Craig Zilles

Oral exams are compelling because (1) students can be asked to provide their reasoning for an answer and (2) their interactive nature permits follow-up questions to resolve ambiguous answers. But oral exams scale poorly. In this paper, we present scalable conversational assessment, an LLM-based system that conducts interactive text-based conversations with students to assess their ability to make and support an argument. We use a two-agent architecture to separate concerns and reduce the likelihood that answers are leaked to the student: the interviewer interacts directly with the student, but only receives guidance about the next question to ask the student; the hidden evaluator knows the goals of the whole activity, tracks the student's progress using a rubric, and guides the interviewer on when to follow-up on the current question or proceed to the next question. The system was first tested using a collection of simulated students with LLM personas of a particular ability level or adversarial tactic (e.g., answer-extractors, prompt-injectors, confident fabricators). A novel element of this evaluation was that it required the use of a fictional assessment topic to prevent personas that shouldn't have information from using knowledge present in the LLM's pre-training. The system was also deployed in a small, elective AI literacy course in both formative and summative contexts. Through manual and post-facto AI review of the transcripts we identified opportunities to improve the follow-up question generation and ensure that grading only credited ideas that the student (and not the interviewer) contributed.

##### **CapLite: Prompt-Injection Defense for Small Language Model Agents without Model-Written Security Artifacts**
2611.17115v1 by Zimo Xu, Wenzhuo Liu, Hideki Nakayama

Enforcement defenses against prompt injection, such as CaMeL, Progent, IPIGuard and DRIFT, ask the agent's own model to write a security artifact (a program, a privilege policy, a dependency graph or a constraint list) that code then enforces. We ask whether small language model (SLM) agents can be trusted to write these artifacts. Across 15 open-weight models, 13 of them between 1.7B and 12B parameters, they cannot: the policies, graphs and constraints that the agent's model writes admit attacks on 14 (Progent), 13 (IPIGuard) and 11 (DRIFT) of the models, and CaMeL's programs fail to run in up to 68% of episodes. We present CapLite, which derives its policy from the tool schemas, commits the write calls before any tool result is read, and admits a write only if every argument that decides whom or what it affects is stated in the user's request. This guarantees write integrity: injected text cannot add a write, and no write reaches a recipient, amount, object or destination that the user did not state. No attack succeeds in 13,935 attacked AgentDojo episodes. CapLite reaches utility under attack comparable to CaMeL's (29.0% vs. 27.2%) with fewer tokens than every other enforcement defense and than the undefended agent: 17.1k per episode, 18% of CaMeL's, and the highest utility per token of all enforcement defenses.

##### **Evaluating Pretrained Language Semantics for Structured EHR Prediction Tasks**
2611.17053v1 by Ashvin Gupta, Brendan Delaney, Alessandra Russo

Structured Electronic Health Records (EHR) models typically treat each clinical concept as a dataset-specific token, discarding the natural-language semantics associated with the event. As a result, relationships between concepts must largely be learned from EHR co-occurrence, without directly exploiting any prior semantic knowledge. We investigate how pretrained causal language models can be adapted for longitudinal EHR prediction by developing tEHR. Here, we represent events with their natural language description and use this text stream to apply EHR-adaptive continued pretraining, followed by task-specific discriminative fine-tuning using LoRA, utilising the LLM as a longitudinal patient-encoder. We evaluate this approach across four prediction tasks on the MIMIC-IV dataset and pancreatic cancer prediction on the CPRD dataset at 1 to 12 month horizons, with tEHR using the Mistral-7B-v0.3 backbone achieving the strongest overall performance. Ablations show that performance declines when clinical descriptions are removed or misaligned, when numerical values are omitted, and when either stage of adaptation is removed, while explicit elapsed-time descriptions provide little additional benefit. Analysis of the learned patient representations further reveals clinically coherent structure associated with distinct patterns of disease risk and clinical history.

##### **AI for Biologists: The Knowledge Boundaries of Foundation Models in the Age of Agentic Systems**
2611.17015v1 by Tian Cai, Andrew Kleist, Jaimin Patel, Duccio Malinverni, Alexander K. Taylor, Malvika Sharan, Renato Umeton, M. Madan Babu

Artificial intelligence (AI) is changing how biological research is done.
