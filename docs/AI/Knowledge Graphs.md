##### **Ambient LLMs: Data-Efficient Supervised Fine-tuning via Temperature Matching**
2611.23229v1 by Giannis Daras, Marios Papachristou, Adam Klivans, Antonio Torralba, Constantinos Daskalakis

Collecting large-scale, diverse, and high-quality post-training datasets for autoregressive language models is challenging. Consequently, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is typically applied to datasets significantly smaller than those used for pre-training, which leads to overfitting and the catastrophic forgetting of the tails of the data distribution. To prevent this collapse, we introduce Ambient SFT, a principled framework for leveraging imperfect predictions to augment the fine-tuning set. In our method, a "frozen" version of the pre-trained model serves as a noisy teacher that the fine-tuned model must match at a coarse level. In particular, we require that the high-temperature versions of the conditional distributions produced by the post-training model should match the high-temperature versions of the pre-training ones. The temperature hyperparameter controls the level of trust in the pre-trained model, with the limit $T\to \infty$ reverting back to regular SFT and $T=1$ to knowledge distillation. We demonstrate that our approach outperforms standard SFT across domain adaptation, instruction-tuning, and reasoning, leading for up to $21\%$ average relative improvement to standard SFT.

##### **WoRD: Diffusion-Based Posterior Inference for Online Goal Recognition**
2611.23192v1 by Almog Anschel, Sarah Keren

Goal recognition is the task of inferring an agent's intended goal from its observed behavior. Most existing work focuses on high-level symbolic recognition, abstracting away the continuous motion through which agents reach their goal. Methods that do reason over continuous trajectories rely on an explicit dynamics model that is hard to acquire and rarely transfers across settings. Neither approach explicitly learns how agents move toward each candidate goal, limiting accuracy on ambiguous trajectory prefixes where such knowledge is most needed.
  We present WoRD (Waypoint Recognition with Diffusion), which uses a learned trajectory prior to infer the agent's goal from partial observations. At each step, WoRD runs one warm-started denoising chain per candidate goal, steers each chain toward the observed trajectory through a closed-form log-likelihood gradient, and reweights the resulting samples by Bayes' rule into a calibrated posterior over goals. We evaluate WoRD on simulated robot settings, pedestrian trajectories, and vehicle trajectories using four metrics targeting distinct failure modes. Across all three settings, the same inference procedure preserves calibrated multimodal beliefs through ambiguous prefixes where filter- and classifier-based observers collapse to a single mode or grow overconfident.

##### **Retrieval-Augmented Correction for Generalist Medical Image Segmentation**
2611.23189v1 by Vincenzo Marcianò, Xiaoming Zhang, Michela Antonelli, Sebastien Ourselin, Maria A Zuluaga

Automated segmentation is routinely used as the first step in quantitative image-based clinical workflows, where predicted masks are subsequently reviewed and corrected by human operators. Automated correction of the residual structural errors is currently addressed one anatomy at a time, so the number of trained correctors scales with the number of segmentation targets. We present GRACE, a retrieval-augmented refiner that edits an input mask under context drawn from a shared knowledge base of verified prior cases. The refiner is trained once on a multi-organ corpus spanning compact organs, cavities, branching vessels, and tumour lesions. In the held-out dataset-transfer evaluation, three held-out datasets query a fixed knowledge base built from training data only. A separate leave-one-anatomy-out experiment tests parameter-free index extension: verified cases of an unseen solid organ enter the knowledge base, and the refiner stays frozen. A light shape-aware projection head on a frozen multimodal encoder maps embedding similarity to the shape agreement of the masks, so the knowledge base returns cases that the refiner can use as a shape prior. We evaluate GRACE on 18 public datasets spanning CT, MRI and ultrasound, using nine refinement configurations and seven upstream segmentors that span weak, mid, and strong operating regimes. We report each change with the DSC of its input mask. On the weak tier, the input DSC is 0.386 and GRACE adds up to +0.35 DSC.
